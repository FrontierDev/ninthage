# 1. Purpose and Authority

This document records **cross-cultural established world history and metaphysics** discussed during cultural development. It provides a shared chronological and causal reference for the [Arhîn](Cultures/Arhin-Culture-PDD.md), [Eldani](Cultures/Eldani-Culture-PDD.md), [Cehîn](Cultures/Cehin-Culture-PDD.md) and [Durhîn](Cultures/Durhin-Culture-PDD.md) culture PDDs. Those culture PDDs remain authoritative for their own social institutions, beliefs, material culture and political structures. [Quest, Narrative and Dialogue](Quest-Narrative-and-Dialogue-PDD.md) owns **gameplay presentation** of this material, not the historical facts themselves.

**Information categories:** **Established truth** (out-of-world fact), **in-world belief or deception** (what characters or societies believe), **current intention** (a character's plan, not a guaranteed outcome) and **open** (not yet decided). Do not promote plans, popular beliefs or proposed interpretations to established truth.

# 2. Cosmology, the Tlen'se and the Silver Key

## 2.1 The Tlen'se — Established truth

Near the beginning of the universe, consciousness arose from quantum fluctuations (a Boltzmann-brain-like origin), eventually becoming stable and able to assume physical forms. The resulting entities have several names in different cultures; the **Lizardfolk** call them the **Tlen'se**. Their morality and nature vary. Both **Y'gdath** and the **Night Serpent** are Tlen'se; the former is malignant and the latter comparatively benevolent.

In prehistoric times Y'gdath arrived to feed upon the minds of the world's sapient magical creatures. They waged war against it and the **ice giant Hrym** killed it. A mountain chain formed where Y'gdath fell; the entity's heart remained buried beneath one mountain. **The Heart of Y'gdath** takes the form of a **silver key** and has many uses to someone who understands it. Its exact capabilities are not exhaustively defined.

## 2.2 Custody of the Key — Established truth

- **Second Age:** a group of humans fleeing the Malamer entered the mountain and found the Heart of Y'gdath, or **Silver Key**.
- **Eighth Age:** **Thainarv Brùn**, a western **Urngor (mountain dwarf) lord**, acquired the Key during an ill-fated expedition into **Uzul Norn**, the unexplored dangerous region south of the **High Peaks**.
- Thainarv did not return home. The Key gave him **unnaturally prolonged life** and **prescience**, and drove him mad.
- No one knew that Thainarv held the Key when the Tinithas later bargained with the Night Serpent; the Key and Thainarv remained missing.
- The Key's disappearance drew the attention of the **Night Serpent**, which dispatched the **Merrshaulk** to recover it and return it to safety. **This began the Serpent War.**

No present-day location for Thainarv or the Key, and no complete list of the Key's powers, has been fixed.

# 3. The Malamer, the Grey and the Twelve

## 3.1 The Malamer — Established truth

The **Malamer** were an advanced magical civilisation present before the beginning of the First Age. They were driven to extinction by the end of the **Sixth Age**. Their leader was the **God-King Osmutuun**.

Before the First Age, Osmutuun and the **Cabal of Twelve** performed a ritual bridging the world and the edge of the universe. The ritual provided access to a realm where nothing yet existed but possibilities were effectively endless. This realm is sometimes called **the Grey**. Osmutuun was betrayed, and the Twelve entered the Grey without him.

The Twelve were originally of Malamer origin. In the Grey they could choose their forms. Their occupancy of that realm conferred effectively unlimited capacity to create and do as they wished. They became known as the **Grey Ones**.

**Godhood in Ninth Age:** broadly, a being of extraordinary magical potency, immortality and capabilities such as differing degrees of telepathy, limited teleportation and prescience. Godhood does not necessarily entail creation from nothing or omnipotence in the physical world.

## 3.2 The courts — Established truth

The Twelve are divided equally into the **Summer Court** and **Winter Court**. Their division is **political**, not a division by fundamental magical schools, moral alignment, season-based powers or origin. All Twelve remain alive and active during the Ninth Age, but are now confined to the physical world by the Terhat-Ilu and consequently have **far less power** than when they occupied the Grey.

The presence of gods on the physical world does not mean they have recovered their former Grey-level freedom or omnipotence.

# 4. Aurësén origins and the Black Winter

## 4.1 Creation of the Aurësén — Established truth

The **Aurësén (Daylight People)** were created through a **prohibited unison of Titramech** (Summer Court) and **Ninathell** (Winter Court). This act and its sapient offspring were viewed among the Grey Ones as dangerous abominations that might threaten their dominion of the Grey. They were effectively banished to the world with instructions to serve as executioners and destroy Osmutuun and the Malamer.

Having gained life and freedom of their own, the Aurësén **chose not to wage war on the established Malamer civilisation for many centuries**. They first entered the world through the **Moonlight Well**; this did **not** mean the Well ever provided a traversable gateway to the Grey.

## 4.2 The Black Winter and the Annokal — Established truth

Because of Titramech's part in their creation, the Aurësén were especially susceptible to sinister and violent aspects of their ancestry. At the end of the **First Age**, **Kabithh** exploited this susceptibility to control them during the **Black Winter**, turning them into hateful, violent beings resembling the fully afflicted Arhîn of the Ninth Age.

The Grey Ones returned to the world to subdue the Aurësén in the ensuing war. At its conclusion, **Auris Koth bestowed the Annokal**, also called the **Gift of Light**, on the Aurësén. Its purpose was specifically **to prevent Titramech's blood being used to control them again**. It granted **neither immortality nor magical potency**.

In the Black Winter, **Faeriden**, an Aurësén, was killed by **Queen Ilvielle**, his lover. The Bow of Faeriden, which Auris Koth had earlier given Faeriden as a safeguard against any one of the Twelve, passed into Kabithh's hands. She altered it so that **she herself is immune to it**.

At the beginning of the **Second Age**, **Queen Ilya** replaced **Queen Ilvielle** and became **High Priestess of Tiandre**. The rulers later called the **Tinithas** received their territorial positions for their resistance to Kabithh. This chronology does not establish precise appointment ceremonies or institutions.

# 5. The Serpent War and the Terhat-Ilu

## 5.1 Night Serpent's mission — Established truth

The Night Serpent originally sought to locate the dangerous Key and secure it, not to destroy the world indiscriminately. The Merrshaulk were dispatched in its name to find the Key and initiated the Serpent War.

## 5.2 The Tinithas' bargain — Established truth and public deception

The three Tinithas are **Iluvantar**, **Histalion** and **Úrien**. Before the **Terhat-Ilu**, they had an audience with the Night Serpent. They agreed that it could **bind the entire world within its own mind for 1,000 years**, during which it would search for the Silver Key.

**The agreed consequence of failure:** once the allotted time had expired, if the Key was not found, the Night Serpent would **destroy the world completely, including the Key**.

**Established truth:** the world was transported and sealed inside the Night Serpent's mind at the end of the Serpent War; the event also caused the world's partial destruction, called the **Terhat-Ilu**.

**The Tinithas' deliberate public lie:** rather than admit that they had permitted the Night Serpent to imprison their world, they said that **they had imprisoned the Night Serpent beneath the Culosíma**. The lie maintained the legitimacy of their decision and the social order. **Kabithh was the one who proposed offering the bargain**. Iluvantar has since come to suspect her involvement in suppressing the Serpent.

**Present status:** **more than 1,000 years have passed**. The Night Serpent has not fulfilled the world-destruction clause because, unknown to the Tinithas, **Kabithh has suppressed the Night Serpent using sylvan blood**. Iluvantar suspects something of this nature but does not know the full truth.

## 5.3 The Ertniiagra — Established truth, belief and intent

The **Ertniiagra** is a **generational centaur pilgrimage and sacrifice** at the Moonlight Well. Centaurs travel from the **western steppes** along a **sacred migration route** to Ytharivandor, where **one chosen western centaur and one chosen eastern centaur** are sacrificed. This rite is deeply important to both the centaurs and the Cehîn.

**Public explanation:** the sacrifices are said to **preserve the binding of the Night Serpent**, consistent with the Tinithas' lie that the Serpent was imprisoned beneath the Culosíma.

**Actual material function:** the rite supplies **sylvan blood** to the Moonlight Well. Sylvan blood is the blood of beings originating from the Grey other than the Arhîn; cited examples include the **Miut** and **centaurs**. The Tinithas believe the process sustains the **world's binding within the Serpent's mind**.

**Hidden mechanism:** Kabithh uses the introduced sylvan blood to maintain her suppression of the Night Serpent. The Tinithas do **not** know this. She currently **needs continued supply** of that blood to retain control.

## 5.4 The Moonlight Well — Established truth vs belief

The Moonlight Well is a **physical natural pool** deep in a **wooded glade**, in **Ytharivandor (the Ancient Forest)** on the southern edge of the Great Forests. It is surrounded by stone of pale-blue **opalescent** quality. Its water has a **silvery surface** and **healing properties**.

It is an ancient **font of magical power linked to the world**, and, since the Terhat-Ilu, by extension to the **Night Serpent**. The Aurësén emerged into the world there. It has **never been a passageway to the Grey**.

**Cehîn pact transformations — established truth:** Myrhufrun's transformative magic and a formal ceremony conducted by Lord Thraen and the Cehîn priesthood initiate the change. **Drinking water from the Moonlight Well completes the transformation**; a candidate who does not drink it becomes a **mindless husk** instead of a conscious Cehîn. This applies to Arhîn newly entering Myrhufrun's pact, as well as to the established nature of Cehîn transformation. The water also leaves the transformed Cehîn vulnerable to **direct control by Kabithh if her planned killing of Myrhufrun succeeds**. **The vulnerability is an inherent consequence of the Well's connection to the Night Serpent**, not the result of Kabithh introducing or altering magic in its waters. Her plan exploits this existing connection. That vulnerability is hidden lore: **neither Lord Thraen nor Myrhufrun knows of it**. The knowledge of other Cehîn or the priesthood has not been established. Its exploitation remains **conditional**, not a present accomplishment.

**Arhîn historical belief:** souls were thought to reach the Grey through the Moonlight Well for reincarnation by a Grey One. **Contemporary Arhîn know that the Well is not an accessible portal to the Grey and do not believe they have a right to enter the Grey.** Their past and present funerary beliefs should be recorded without asserting that reincarnation actually occurs.

# 6. Yer-Aranie, the Annokal and the surviving Grey Ones

## 6.1 The Sanctum of Starlight — Established truth

At the end of the Serpent War, **Ythralla** (Queen of the Winter Court) and **Auris Koth** (Prince of Daylight and Ythralla's lover) fought the Merrshaulk in **Yer-Aranie**. **Kabithh** arrived with **Sszeraj**, a Serpent Priest bearing the **Bow of Faeriden**, intending for Sszeraj to kill Ythralla.

Sszeraj fired at Ythralla. Auris Koth stepped in front of her. When the arrow struck him, Ythralla **froze the chamber**, preventing his death. Kabithh then used **telekinesis** to drive the arrow deeper. Auris Koth **transposed his spirit out of his body into the surrounding ice**, preventing its destruction.

Kabithh cursed all Yer-Aranie to die, forcing Ythralla to witness its destruction. **Auris Koth's incapacitation**, not Kabithh's curse itself, caused the **Annokal to cease functioning**, exposing the Aurësén and their Arhîn descendants to the **Withering**.

Ythralla remains in the ice of the **Sanctum of Starlight**, consciously aware. She **deliberately sustains the freezing**: releasing the ice without first restoring Auris Koth would kill him. She knows his spirit has been removed from the ice and that the ring containing it could be used to reunite him with his body through magic **at Yer-Aranie**. She cannot simply unfreeze the chamber to recover the ring.

**Kabithh believes Auris Koth's spirit remains inside the ice.** Queen Ilya and the Tinithas believe Auris Koth is permanently lost and do not know he can be restored. **Restoring him at Yer-Aranie would reactivate the Annokal and free the Arhîn from the Withering.** The detailed spell or exact means of reunion are undefined.

## 6.2 The Bow of Faeriden — Established truth

Auris Koth gave Faeriden the Bow when the Aurësén first entered the world. It was designed as the **only weapon capable of killing a Grey One**, a safeguard against any one of the Twelve. Kabithh later modified it to make herself immune. Sszeraj was killed after the events in the Sanctum.

An **unidentified person** managed to enter the Sanctum after the catastrophe, retrieve the Bow and bind Auris Koth's displaced spirit into a **ring**. Who performed the recovery, when, and with what means is **open**.

In the Ninth Age:
- **Messa**, a **male Miut assassin**, holds the Bow. He knows it can kill a Grey One but does **not** know Kabithh is immune. He wishes to do good and, by the planned confrontation, knows what Kabithh represents.
- **Czera**, a **female Kiram (tiefling) occultist**, holds the ring. She knows it contains a soul but cannot communicate with it and does **not** know it is Auris Koth.
- **Kabithh deliberately placed the Bow in Faeriden's mausoleum** where Messa would find it. She knows Messa possesses it.

## 6.3 Kabithh's current objective — Established intentions, not accomplished results

Kabithh seeks the **Silver Key** for herself. She intends to gain total control of the Night Serpent, **envelop the world in darkness**, trigger a **second Black Winter** and raise an army to scour the world for the Key.

**Myrhufrun**, the Stag King of the Summer Court, lives beside the Moonlight Well among his Cehîn worshippers. Kabithh is engineering his death: she plans to **appear to Messa visibly but not to Myrhufrun**, positioning herself before the Stag King so that Messa fires at her. Because the Bow **cannot harm Kabithh**, the arrow would pass through her and **kill Myrhufrun**, spilling his blood into the Well.

**Expected results:** Myrhufrun's blood could give Kabithh effectively **permanent control of the Night Serpent**, ending her dependence on continued Ertniiagra sacrifices. **Separately, the Cehîn's consumption of Moonlight Well water during their transformations would enable Kabithh to exercise direct control over them if she succeeds in killing Myrhufrun.** This is a consequence of their completed pact transformation and a distinct vulnerability from the blood's effect on the Night Serpent. Both are contingent on **Kabithh's planned assassination of the Stag King**; neither is an event that has already occurred. **Her prospective control over the Cehîn would be complete domination of their minds and actions, not merely emotional influence or persuasion with residual free will. Under this control they would become mindless puppets incapable of independent thought**, rather than retaining their intelligence, memories and individual personalities as conscious but compelled subjects. This is distinct from the failed pact transformation that produces a mindless husk before the Well's water is consumed. The exact mechanism, outward manifestations and whether their previous mental faculties could be restored if domination ended remain unspecified.

# 7. Cross-cultural relationships — Established anchors

- **Arhîn:** descendants of Aurësén, still vulnerable to the Withering due to the incapacitated Annokal. Their organised worship recognises both Courts, though Kabithh is an enemy and her worship is heavily forbidden.
- **Durhîn (Dark Children):** Aurësén descendants who pledged themselves to Kabithh and partially embraced the Withering; they retain bodies but experience persistent negative, typically violent emotions. They have a northern tundra/taiga civilisation, capital **Hara-Ur**, and are deeply hostile to Arhîn.
- **Cehîn (Weald Children):** former Aurësén of Ytharivandor transformed through Lord Thraen's pact with Myrhufrun, avoiding Withering at the price of service and safeguarding the Ancient Forest. They revere Myrhufrun and maintain the Well settlement **Te'tutivae**.
- **Eldani:** humans. Their faith **Mahlism** reveres **Mahlfach**, who was in fact **Osmutuun**; the Eldani do not know this. Orthodox and Astonian interpretations differ substantially.
- **Estyenni:** an independent northern people whose secession from Astonia occurred during the reign of Achov III's father.
- **Miut, centaurs:** explicitly cited as sylvan peoples whose blood is relevant to the Moonlight Well, but their cultures and biological distinctions are not fully defined here.

# 8. Chronological reference — Established, without invented dates

| Period | Events |

| Before First Age | Malamer God-King Osmutuun and Cabal of Twelve open passage to the Grey; the Twelve betray Osmutuun and enter it |
| Beginning of First Age | Titramech and Ninathell's Aurësén enter the world from Moonlight Well, initially sent to destroy the Malamer |
| End of First Age | Black Winter; Grey Ones intervene; Auris Koth bestows the Annokal |
| Start of Second Age | Queen Ilya replaces Queen Ilvielle and becomes Tiandre's High Priestess; Tinithas rewarded for resisting Kabithh |
| Second Age | Humans fleeing the Malamer discover the Heart of Y'gdath, the Silver Key |

| Seventh–Eighth Ages | Osmutuun imprisoned |
| Eighth Age | Thainarv Brùn discovers Key in Uzul Norn; Night Serpent dispatches Merrshaulk |
| End of Serpent War / Terhat-Ilu | Tinithas bargain for 1,000 years; world bound inside Night Serpent's mind; Yer-Aranie cursed; Auris Koth incapacitated; Annokal fails; Withering begins |

Osmutuun returned to his former place of imprisonment **after the Serpent War**, distancing himself from worldly events. Do not infer that the various Ages were equal in length.

# 9. Open lore, continuity and disclosure

- Identity, name and title of the **sixth Winter Court member**.
- The anonymous person who recovered the Bow and ring from the Sanctum, and the circumstances of recovery.
- Full mechanics of the Key, of permanent suppression and of Auris Koth's magical restoration.
- Where Thainarv and the Silver Key are now.
- The exact mechanics and provenance of the Annokal and the consequences of restoring it beyond ending Withering.
- Nature, organisation and precise role of the Grey beyond the original Twelve's experience; whether reincarnation ever operated as Arhîn theology imagined is not established.
- Identity of the original western/eastern centaur sacrificial candidates, historical Ertniiagra chronology and ritual personnel.
- Exact present whereabouts and activities of many individual Grey Ones; all Twelve are alive and active, but not all are physically located.
- The alternative spelling **Iluvanatar** appears in later discussion; **Iluvantar** is used in earlier territorial-ruler notes. Standardisation should be confirmed rather than silently changing names.
- The precise history of Aurësén political institutions, their diarchy, and the Malamer's collapse remain incomplete.

**Continuity rules:** distinguish **the world bound inside the Night Serpent** (truth) from **the Night Serpent bound beneath the Culosíma** (Tinithas' lie), and **sylvan blood enabling Kabithh's suppression** (hidden truth) from **sacrifice maintaining an imprisoned Serpent** (public doctrine). The ritual and the proposed Stag King assassination are distinct sources of blood. **The Well water required to complete Cehîn transformations is also the source of their hidden vulnerability to Kabithh's direct control, conditional on Myrhufrun's death; this must not be confused with the blood-related Night Serpent mechanism.** The Winter and Summer Courts are political, not intrinsically moral categories.
