# Ninth Age — Business, Publicity and Funding Roadmap

**Status:** Living non-game project roadmap  
**Project:** Ninth Age  
**Branch:** `dev`  
**Scope:** Website, public presence, community, business/legal preparation, funding, publisher/investor outreach, store presence, press, playtests and launch-facing operations  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-07

---

## 1. Purpose

This document is the non-game companion to the [Project Roadmap](Project-Roadmap.md).

It covers work that sits outside direct game implementation but is necessary to turn Ninth Age from a private development project into a fundable, marketable and operable commercial MMORPG.

The roadmap is **milestone-gated rather than date-gated**.

Publicity and fundraising should happen when the project can support the claims being made about it. Ninth Age should not attempt to market the scale of an MMORPG before it can demonstrate the core technology, art direction and gameplay loop credibly.

---

## 2. Core Principles

1. **Do not spend credibility before the project can support it.**
2. **Build discoverability early, but delay major promotion until there is strong material to show.**
3. **A polished vertical slice is the main funding threshold.**
4. **Do not use crowdfunding to finance an unproven prototype.**
5. **Do not expand the team significantly before production pipelines and technical foundations are stable.**
6. **Own the project's identity, domains, accounts, source code and key IP cleanly before outside funding or publishing discussions.**
7. **Collect an audience in channels the project controls, especially an email list.**
8. **Public communication should show working systems and authored content rather than promises about eventual MMO scale.**
9. **Funding decisions should be based on the next demonstrable milestone, not the total theoretical cost of building the entire MMORPG.**
10. **Keep public commitments narrower than internal ambitions until the production capacity exists to deliver them.**

# External Phase 0 — Quiet Foundation

**Aligned game phases:** Project Roadmap Phase 0–1  
**Public posture:** Low-key / unannounced development

## Goal

Establish the project's commercial identity and basic public infrastructure without beginning a major publicity campaign.

## Website

Create a simple official website with:

- project name and logo/wordmark;
- one-paragraph description of Ninth Age;
- a small number of representative screenshots or concept images only when they accurately represent the project;
- development-status wording that makes clear the game is in early development;
- mailing-list signup;
- links to official social/community accounts;
- contact address;
- privacy policy where data is collected.

The website should initially be a **clean landing page**, not a content-heavy marketing site.

Reserve the domain and relevant common misspellings/variants where sensible.

## Identity and account ownership

Secure project-controlled ownership of:

- primary domain;
- source-control organisation;
- primary email domain;
- social accounts;
- video channel;
- community server;
- future storefront/publisher-facing accounts where appropriate.

Use shared project/business ownership rather than personal accounts for assets that may later need to survive staff changes.

## Business/legal hygiene

Before taking meaningful outside money:

- decide the legal entity that will own Ninth Age;
- ensure source code, art, music, writing and other project assets have clear ownership;
- use written contributor/contractor agreements;
- maintain third-party licence records;
- maintain an asset provenance register;
- review the project name for obvious trademark conflicts before investing heavily in branding;
- establish basic bookkeeping and project cost tracking.

Formal trademark filing can follow once the project name and commercial intent are sufficiently settled, but clearance should happen earlier.

## Public presence

Create but do not aggressively promote:

- official website;
- mailing list;
- one or two primary social channels;
- video channel;
- Discord or equivalent community space only if there is enough activity to justify moderating it.

Avoid opening many inactive channels merely for completeness.

## Development communication

Suitable material at this stage:

- occasional technical/dev progress;
- world-building/art-direction material;
- short clips of genuinely working systems;
- tooling/world-generation demonstrations where visually understandable.

Avoid:

- release dates;
- detailed player-count promises;
- promises of specific launch scope;
- aggressive comparisons with established MMORPGs;
- announcements framed as though the project is close to release.

## Funding

### Appropriate now

- founder/self-funding;
- small private investment from people who understand the risk;
- relevant grants or non-dilutive support;
- small technology/art-development funding opportunities;
- conversations with potential advisers or future partners.

### Usually premature now

- broad publisher pitching;
- institutional investment;
- large angel rounds;
- public crowdfunding campaigns.

The project is more likely to receive useful terms once it can demonstrate a complete gameplay loop.

## Exit milestone — Credible Private Project

Ninth Age has a clean identity, official web presence, mailing-list capture, clear IP ownership and cost records, while game development reaches the Durable Character / early vertical-slice stage.

# External Phase 1 — Demonstrable Project

**Aligned game phase:** Project Roadmap Phase 2  
**Public posture:** Controlled visibility

## Goal

Turn Ninth Age into something that can be shown coherently to outsiders.

The key asset is the **Core Playable Vertical Slice**.

## Website expansion

Upgrade the landing page into a compact project site containing:

- game overview;
- design pillars;
- selected screenshots;
- short gameplay footage;
- development updates;
- mailing-list signup;
- FAQ;
- press/business contact.

Do not publish an enormous feature catalogue. The site should explain what is already credible and what makes Ninth Age distinctive.

## Public development material

Begin a regular but sustainable cadence of:

- short gameplay clips;
- environment/world clips;
- combat examples;
- behind-the-scenes development posts;
- selected technical demonstrations where they communicate player value;
- longer devlogs only when there is enough substance to justify them.

A consistent monthly update is more valuable than frequent low-information posts.

## Trailer material

Produce a short **development/vertical-slice video**, not necessarily a commercial launch trailer.

It should demonstrate:

- multiplayer movement;
- the open-world presentation;
- combat;
- NPCs;
- character progression;
- inventory/equipment;
- server-backed persistence;
- seamless or streamed world behaviour where visually demonstrable.

Avoid a montage that conceals the actual game loop.

## Community

Begin deliberately growing:

- mailing list;
- Discord/community;
- video followers;
- social followers.

The mailing list should remain the primary owned audience.

Track:

- subscriber growth;
- engagement;
- video completion/retention;
- Discord active users rather than raw member count;
- recurring questions and feature interest.

## First external playtests

Run small controlled playtests with people who were not involved in development.

Measure:

- onboarding comprehension;
- technical failures;
- latency/network behaviour;
- combat comprehension;
- whether players can identify a meaningful game loop;
- session length;
- reasons for stopping;
- strongest/weakest perceived features.

Do not optimise for vanity player counts yet.

## Funding preparation

Prepare a proper funding package:

- concise pitch deck;
- playable build;
- vertical-slice video;
- product positioning;
- technical architecture summary;
- current team/capabilities;
- production roadmap;
- milestone-based budget;
- use of funds;
- staffing plan;
- key risks;
- existing audience/traction;
- ownership/IP summary.

The budget should distinguish between:

- minimum funding needed to reach the next major milestone;
- realistic production funding;
- longer-term full MMO ambitions.

## Exit milestone — Pitchable Vertical Slice

A third party can understand Ninth Age by playing or watching it without needing the developer to explain away missing fundamentals.

# External Phase 2 — Funding and Formal Announcement

**Aligned game phases:** Project Roadmap Phase 2 exit through Phase 3–4  
**Public posture:** Announced project

## Goal

Use a credible vertical slice and representative world material to secure the resources needed for real production.

## Funding outreach threshold

**This is the preferred point for serious publisher/investor outreach.**

Do not wait until the project needs money urgently.

Begin outreach while there is sufficient runway to:

- continue development during negotiations;
- reject poor terms;
- answer diligence requests;
- improve the build between pitch rounds.

## Funding targets

Potential routes may include:

- specialist game publishers;
- strategic development partners;
- angel investors with games experience;
- game-focused venture investors where the scale justifies it;
- non-dilutive grants;
- platform funding;
- regional creative/technology funding.

Treat each as a different relationship rather than sending the same pitch indiscriminately.

## Pitch emphasis

The funding case should centre on demonstrated de-risking:

- working headless client/server architecture;
- working multiplayer;
- world streaming;
- credible combat loop;
- persistent character state;
- scalable authoring direction;
- distinctive product/design identity;
- evidence that the project is more than a collection of MMO design documents.

The pitch should also be explicit about the risks and cost of content production and live operation.

## Funding ask

Prefer a milestone-based ask large enough to reach a materially stronger state.

A weak raise that funds only a few months of additional prototyping may create fundraising pressure without changing the project's investability.

Likely funded work at this stage includes:

- production art;
- environment/content team;
- animation;
- additional engineering;
- tools/content pipeline;
- backend/operations;
- audio;
- UI/UX;
- QA;
- early production staffing.

## Public announcement

A formal public announcement becomes appropriate when Ninth Age has:

- a strong visual identity;
- representative gameplay footage;
- a stable vertical slice;
- a website capable of converting interest into mailing-list signups;
- enough development confidence to answer basic questions publicly.

Announcement assets should include:

- announcement trailer/gameplay video;
- key art;
- screenshot set;
- concise description;
- website;
- FAQ;
- press kit;
- business/press contact.

## Press kit

Create a downloadable press kit containing:

- logo files;
- key art;
- screenshots;
- fact sheet;
- short and long descriptions;
- developer/studio information;
- contact details;
- approved gameplay footage/B-roll if useful.

## Store presence

Create a PC storefront page—especially the Steam page—once:

- the visual presentation is representative;
- the project is publicly announced;
- screenshots/trailer are strong enough not to damage first impressions;
- the team can sustain ongoing wishlist-driving activity.

The store page should come **early enough to accumulate wishlists for a long period**, but not so early that the first impression is a rough prototype.

## Exit milestone — Fundable / Public Production Project

Ninth Age has either secured production funding or has enough demonstrated traction and runway to continue independently, and public interest is being captured through the website, mailing list and storefront.

# External Phase 3 — Production Audience Building

**Aligned game phases:** Project Roadmap Phase 3–5  
**Public posture:** Active development

## Goal

Build a real audience while the project proves its world, dungeon, economy and social systems.

## Website

Expand the site only where useful:

- development news;
- media gallery;
- class/world features as they become representative;
- playtest signup;
- newsletter archive;
- FAQ;
- press kit;
- support/contact pathways.

Avoid maintaining a large lore/wiki site before there is enough final content to justify it.

## Content strategy

Primary content categories:

- gameplay demonstrations;
- world/environment showcases;
- class/combat showcases;
- dungeon development;
- profession/crafting showcases;
- technical features translated into player-facing benefits;
- developer commentary;
- controlled playtest highlights.

Show progress through **before → implementation → playable result** where useful.

## Influencers/creators

Begin building relationships with:

- MMO-focused creators;
- RPG-focused creators;
- Unity/game-development creators where technical material is appropriate;
- smaller creators willing to engage deeply with early builds.

Do not rely only on large creators. Smaller relevant communities can provide better early feedback and conversion.

## Playtests

Increase playtest scope gradually:

1. trusted external testers;
2. invite-only community tests;
3. larger scheduled alpha tests.

Maintain clear confidentiality rules where needed.

Collect:

- retention;
- session length;
- completion funnels;
- crash/error rates;
- latency/network quality;
- class/content preferences;
- qualitative feedback;
- support burden.

## Community operations

As the community becomes active:

- publish community rules;
- define moderation processes;
- establish support/reporting routes;
- recruit moderation help only when needed;
- separate development discussion from support channels;
- avoid letting Discord become the only source of project information.

## Funding follow-on

Seek additional funding only when a new milestone materially changes the project's value or funding requirement.

Examples:

- complete representative zone;
- functioning five-player dungeon;
- proven social/economy loop;
- meaningful wishlist/community traction;
- successful multiplayer alpha.

Avoid continual fundraising that distracts from production.

## Exit milestone — Proven Production

Ninth Age has a representative complete zone, real multiplayer content, repeated external playtests and an audience that engages with the project beyond announcement curiosity.

# External Phase 4 — Alpha Commercialisation

**Aligned game phases:** Project Roadmap Phase 6–8  
**Public posture:** Playable alpha

## Goal

Convert awareness into sustained interest and prove that Ninth Age can operate as an online game.

## Alpha strategy

Move from occasional tests toward structured alpha periods.

Possible progression:

- closed technical alpha;
- closed gameplay/content alpha;
- larger invitation waves;
- persistent or semi-persistent test environment where operationally useful.

Do not call a build an alpha merely for publicity; the phase should test representative game systems and service operation.

## Store/wishlist campaign

Increase wishlist activity through:

- major gameplay updates;
- trailers;
- demo/test announcements;
- developer broadcasts;
- store news posts;
- creator coverage;
- relevant platform events where eligibility and build quality make sense.

Track conversion rather than raw impressions.

## Public relations

At major milestones, conduct targeted outreach to:

- MMO press;
- PC gaming press;
- RPG press;
- relevant newsletters/podcasts;
- creators.

Use a specific news hook:

- first major gameplay reveal;
- alpha signup;
- first dungeon;
- world-scale reveal;
- major funding/publishing partnership;
- release window.

Avoid generic “development continues” press pitching.

## Community staffing

Consider dedicated part-time/full-time community support when:

- community volume materially interrupts developers;
- regular public testing requires moderation/support;
- response times and communication consistency matter commercially.

## Business operations

Before accepting substantial public testing:

- privacy/data handling should be formalised;
- terms/EULA should be prepared as required;
- support processes should exist;
- incident response should be defined;
- payment/refund obligations should be understood before selling anything;
- operational costs should be measured against realistic player counts.

## Exit milestone — Closed Alpha Ready

The game can host meaningful external populations, collect useful telemetry/feedback, support users operationally and sustain a regular public communications cadence.

# External Phase 5 — Beta and Launch Campaign

**Aligned game phase:** Project Roadmap Phase 9  
**Public posture:** Pre-release

## Goal

Turn an established interested audience into launch demand while verifying live operations.

## Marketing assets

Prepare final-quality:

- gameplay trailer;
- launch/feature trailer;
- screenshots;
- key art;
- storefront assets;
- website;
- class/world feature pages where useful;
- press kit;
- creator B-roll;
- launch FAQ.

## Beta

Use beta to test:

- scale;
- operations;
- onboarding;
- retention;
- economy;
- support;
- client compatibility;
- patching;
- server deployment;
- real-world hardware/network conditions.

A beta is not primarily a substitute for unfinished core development.

## Creator/press access

Provide controlled preview access once the game can withstand public scrutiny.

Supply:

- clear embargo/review conditions where applicable;
- test schedules;
- known-issues notes;
- direct support contact;
- capture guidance only where technically necessary.

Do not attempt to dictate opinions.

## Launch timing

Do not set a public launch date until the project has credible confidence in:

- content completion;
- server scalability;
- persistence;
- patching;
- operations;
- critical bug rate;
- staffing/support capacity.

A release window can be communicated earlier than an exact date.

## Paid marketing

Large paid campaigns should come late relative to organic/community building.

Use paid spend when:

- the store page converts;
- trailers/screenshots are representative;
- the release window is credible;
- wishlist-to-purchase assumptions can be tested;
- there is enough budget to learn and iterate rather than make one large blind spend.

## Exit milestone — Release Candidate

Ninth Age has a measurable launch audience, tested operations, final commercial presentation, mature support/community processes and no dependency on unproven funding assumptions for immediate survival.

# External Phase 6 — Launch and Live Service

**Aligned game phase:** Project Roadmap Phase 10

## Goal

Operate Ninth Age as a long-term product rather than treating launch as the end of development.

## Launch operations

Maintain:

- status communication;
- support;
- moderation;
- incident response;
- patch notes;
- hotfix process;
- release/change logs;
- server-capacity monitoring;
- database/backups;
- community communication.

## Public communication

After launch, communication should prioritise:

- service status;
- fixes;
- balance changes;
- content roadmap;
- upcoming events;
- new content;
- transparent handling of significant incidents.

## Post-launch marketing

Use major content updates as renewed acquisition moments.

Potential beats include:

- major zones;
- dungeons/raids;
- class content;
- PvP seasons;
- world events;
- profession/economy updates;
- expansions.

## Funding/reinvestment

Prefer growth funded by operating revenue where viable.

If external capital is used post-launch, it should have a clear purpose such as:

- expansion;
- platform release;
- major content increase;
- infrastructure scaling;
- acquisition/user-growth campaign.

# 3. Funding Gates

The following gates should guide fundraising decisions.

| Project state | Funding posture |
|---|---|
| Prototype / architecture cleanup | Self-fund, grants, small non-dilutive opportunities; avoid broad investor/publisher campaign |
| Durable character / early slice | Begin relationships and research; prepare materials |
| Polished vertical slice | **Primary publisher/investor outreach point** |
| Complete representative zone + dungeon | Strong second funding point; substantially more de-risked |
| Closed alpha with traction | Growth/production follow-on funding can use real player data |
| Beta | Funding should no longer be compensating for unproven fundamentals |
| Live game | Capital should finance growth, not basic product viability |

---

# 4. Crowdfunding Gate

Crowdfunding should be considered only when all of the following are true:

- the game already has a convincing playable build;
- representative gameplay can be shown without qualification;
- production costs and remaining scope are understood;
- the project has an existing audience rather than expecting the campaign to create one from nothing;
- reward obligations can be fulfilled without damaging development;
- the funding target is enough to achieve a meaningful milestone;
- campaign failure would not automatically kill the project.

For Ninth Age, crowdfunding is more appropriate **after the vertical slice and initial audience-building period**, not during the current architecture/prototype stage.

# 6. Publicity Roadmap

## Before vertical slice

Publicity should be **development visibility**, not a marketing campaign.

Primary objective:

> Establish that Ninth Age exists and collect interested followers without creating expectations the current build cannot satisfy.

## At vertical slice

Primary objective:

> Prove the game is real.

This is the first point where coordinated gameplay footage, developer outreach and funding conversations become valuable.

## At representative-zone/dungeon stage

Primary objective:

> Prove the game can scale from prototype systems into actual MMORPG content.

This is a strong point for broader press, creators and a storefront/wishlist campaign.

## At alpha

Primary objective:

> Prove players want to return.

Retention, repeat play and community activity become more important than raw reach.

## At beta

Primary objective:

> Convert established interest into launch demand.

# 7. Business Metrics to Track

Avoid managing the project by social follower count alone.

Useful external/commercial metrics include:

### Audience

- mailing-list subscribers;
- newsletter open/click rates;
- storefront wishlists;
- wishlist growth by campaign;
- Discord monthly active users;
- video watch time/completion;
- website signup conversion.

### Playtests

- invited → installed;
- installed → played;
- median session length;
- repeat-session rate;
- technical failure rate;
- onboarding completion;
- content completion;
- qualitative satisfaction.

### Production/business

- monthly burn;
- runway;
- contractor/staff cost;
- infrastructure cost;
- estimated server cost per concurrent user;
- content production rate;
- cost per asset/content unit where measurable.

### Funding

- introductions;
- meetings;
- follow-up requests;
- diligence stage;
- reasons for rejection;
- proposed terms;
- capital required to reach the next milestone.

# 8. Team Expansion Gates

Do not scale headcount simply because the project is ambitious.

## Before vertical slice

Keep the team small.

Use contractors selectively for areas where a specialist can produce a reusable foundation:

- branding/key visual work;
- concept art;
- character/environment art tests;
- animation tests;
- audio;
- legal/accounting.

## After vertical slice

Funding may justify targeted hires for:

- gameplay/client engineering;
- server/backend engineering;
- environment/content production;
- character/animation;
- technical art;
- UI/UX;
- tools;
- design/content implementation.

## During production

Add production capacity based on measured pipeline constraints.

## Alpha onward

Add:

- QA;
- community/support;
- live operations;
- additional backend/operations capacity where load requires it.

---

# 9. Immediate Non-Game Priorities

The current priorities should be:

1. secure/confirm the Ninth Age domain and official project accounts;
2. create a minimal official website with mailing-list capture;
3. establish clean project/business ownership of code and assets;
4. maintain a licence/provenance register for third-party assets;
5. begin basic project bookkeeping, burn and runway tracking;
6. reserve public channels but avoid a major announcement campaign;
7. collect strong screenshots/video opportunistically as the vertical slice improves;
8. prepare a press-kit structure but do not aggressively distribute it yet;
9. research relevant grants/publishers/investors and build a private contact list;
10. **delay serious funding outreach until the Phase-2 playable vertical slice is presentable**, unless an unusually suitable non-dilutive opportunity appears earlier.

# 10. Relationship to the Game Roadmap

The critical commercial sequence is:

```text
Stable Foundation
    ↓
Durable Character
    ↓
Playable MMORPG Loop
    ↓
SERIOUS FUNDING / PUBLIC ANNOUNCEMENT GATE
    ↓
One Complete Zone
    ↓
Five-Player Dungeon
    ↓
STORE / WISHLIST / BROADER PUBLICITY ACCELERATION
    ↓
World Alpha
    ↓
CLOSED ALPHA
    ↓
Endgame + Hardening
    ↓
BETA / LAUNCH CAMPAIGN
    ↓
Release Candidate
    ↓
LIVE SERVICE
```

The project should enter each publicity/funding stage because the game has earned the next claim it needs to make.
