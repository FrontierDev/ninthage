# 1. Purpose and Authority

This is the dedicated **Arhîn** cultural sub-PDD, under the [Culture Design PDD](../Culture-Design-PDD.md). It is the canonical place for Arhîn-specific history, society, religion, geography as cultural context, art direction and questions awaiting decisions.

Its purpose is to define an identity that can inform environment art, architecture, props, clothing, armour, characters and visual storytelling without either repeating the Eldani style or drifting into generic elven fantasy.

Decision labels follow the parent:
- **Established:** explicitly supplied lore or directly supported by existing game data. Where they differ, both are recorded and the discrepancy flagged.
- **Proposed:** interpretative visual direction suitable for concepting, **not** new canon.
- **Further Consideration Required:** unresolved; do not silently invent implementation or lore decisions.

The [Graphical Approach PDD](../../Graphical-Approach-PDD.md) controls the project's grounded mid-poly visual fidelity, modularity, materials, concept presentation, shaders and asset construction. Cultural description does not create character gameplay, Withering mechanics, faction rules or final maps.

# 2. Arhîn (Dawn Children)

## 2.1 Core Identity — Established

The **Arhîn**, the **Dawn Children**, are descendants of the **Aurësén**, the **Daylight People**. **The Arhîn are a playable race**; continued cultural design should prioritise matters that materially inform playable identity and the player experience, while preserving the distinction between cultural lore and game mechanics.

The Aurësén lost the **Gift of Light** at the end of the **Serpent War**, approximately **1,000 years ago**. Their descendants continue an ancient and exceptionally magical civilisation, but now depend on regular contact with **sunlight or magical sources of power** to avoid the Withering.

The Arhîn are **functionally immortal** but **do not naturally possess godlike powers**. Longevity is not equivalent to divinity, invulnerability or exemption from the Withering.

Their mainstream society revolves around a three-caste hierarchy, material dependence on protectors, concentrated magical authority and the management of access to light; naturalists occupy a socially distinct outsider caste. Their security rests primarily on **diplomacy, espionage and the deterrent of extraordinary magical power**, rather than a standing military.

**Defining visual premise:** luminous, elevated privilege above an ancient forest world, with progressively darker and more dependent forms of habitation at lower levels. The civilisation is both magnificent and vulnerable. **Its present splendour is the diminished inheritance of a more accomplished Aurësén civilisation, not a new golden age.**

## 2.2 Historical Foundation — Established

- The Aurësén are the Arhîn's ancestors.
- The Serpent War ended approximately 1,000 years ago and culminated in the **loss of the Gift of Light** and the destruction of **Yer-Aranie**, a great Aurësén city.
- Following this loss, the Arhîn became susceptible to **the Withering**.
- Over the subsequent millennium, Arhîn architectural expertise and the ability to construct their grandest buildings have **degraded**: master designers and craftsmen died in the Serpent War or were later lost to the Withering.
- Despite retaining a highly sophisticated civilisation and splendid buildings, **contemporary Arhîn architecture is generally less accomplished than architecture from the Aurësén peak**. Only the highest rulers retain truly opulent abodes.
- The present Arhîn primarily occupy the **three Great Forests** around the **Culosíma**.
- Existing race data identifies the ancestral realm as the **Aurësén Diarchy**, with Arhîn lands continuing part of its former western territory.
- **Terhat-Ilu** is the catastrophe at the Serpent War's end, when the world was bound inside the Night Serpent's mind. **Annokal** is the established name for the Gift of Light. It protects Aurësén from control via Titramech's blood, not from ageing or weak magical ability; it failed when Auris Koth was incapacitated.

**Important:** the Grey Ones' origins, creation of Aurësén, the Black Winter, the Annokal and the Terhat-Ilu are established in [World Lore and History](../World-Lore-and-History-PDD.md). The Aurësén Diarchy's full politics, Yer-Aranie's status as capital or otherwise, and the fate of every Aurësén community remain unresolved.

## 2.3 Nostalgia and Public Memory-Viewing Scriptures — Established / Proposed

### Cultural nostalgia — Established

The Arhîn are **inherently nostalgic** for their ancestral civilisation and the times of the **Aurësén**. Reflecting on ancient times is a **common pastime**, not merely an occasional scholarly or religious activity.

Public spaces contain **magical runic scriptures** whose patterns induce **trances and memory viewing** in Arhîn who contemplate them. To an outsider, the practice can seem strange: the Arhîn appear simply to **stand or sit staring at the runic patterns**, while the experience of remembering unfolds inwardly.

The contrast between their outward stillness and inward engagement with the lost past should be central to their everyday cultural portrayal. Nostalgia must not be treated as a symptom of the Withering: it is an established characteristic of Arhîn culture in its own right.

This information does **not** establish whether the memories are first-hand recollections, preserved Aurësén records, inherited/collective memories or magically constructed representations. The precise mechanism, historical accuracy, and whether outsiders can experience the trance remain open. The practice does not, by itself, establish worship of the runes or any protective effect against the Withering.

### Public-space and visual direction — Proposed

- Incorporate runic scriptures into **ordinary shared public spaces**—for example, along squares, galleries, passages or sheltered gathering areas—rather than reserving them exclusively for religious sites or elite libraries.
- Present the scriptures as carefully authored **legible patterned surfaces**: inset stone or metal panels, engraved walls or freestanding tablets are possible forms, not yet fixed designs.
- Arrange some public spaces to allow people to pause and contemplate the patterns without obstructing circulation. These places may form quiet counterpoints to Arhîn towers, markets and workshops.
- Use restrained ambient effects around the scriptures; the primary observable behaviour should remain an Arhîn character **quietly gazing at the runes**. A large holographic projection visible to every bystander would undermine the established outsider's perspective.
- Depict the experience through still poses, subtle shifts in posture and sustained attention rather than treating every trance as a dramatic magical ceremony.
- If memory-viewing scenes are ever visualised to the player, their form and narrative reliability require a separate approved presentation design.

**Design distinction:** these runic scriptures are a **widespread cultural medium for memory viewing**. Their relationship to woodland shrines, Tiandre, the Grey Ones, magic-sourced protection and the three-caste hierarchy is not yet established.

## 2.4 The Withering — Established

The Withering is a consequence of the loss of the Gift of Light. **Prolonged withdrawal from sunlight or magical sources of power** causes:
1. increasingly deep negative and violent emotions;
2. worsening emotional instability and, eventually, physical degradation;
3. skin becoming progressively **transparent**;
4. the body disappearing altogether, leaving a **violent, hysterical shadow**.

The Withering is not ordinary ageing. The Arhîn's functional immortality does not protect them from this fate.

**The Arhîn initially regarded the Withering as a terrible injustice inflicted on their people. Over the course of the Ninth Age, however, some Arhîn have come to believe that it was a deserved consequence of the Aurësén's own actions.** This is a developing religious and cultural interpretation, **not an established objective judgement** on whether the Withering was deserved. The change has contributed to **the rise of fey cults across both the Summer and Winter Courts** among the Arhîn, reflecting a broader religious transformation rather than a movement confined to Winter Court worship. Their affiliations with individual deities, beliefs, rites, organisation and place within or outside the established priesthoods remain to be defined. The newer belief does not replace the original interpretation across Arhîn society.

**Restoration of Auris Koth and reactivation of the Annokal makes a remedy for the Withering available across all Aurësén-derived peoples**, including the Arhîn, Cehîn and Durhîn. **The Cehîn and Durhîn must each accept the Gift to receive its protection or healing**; reactivation does not automatically cure these peoples, notwithstanding their divergent later pacts and transformations. **For those who accept, protection from the Withering is immediate, while their distinctive physical changes reverse gradually and they regain their original Aurësén appearance.** The exact recovery process for individuals already severely affected has not been defined. No exposure duration, severity thresholds, rate of progression or gameplay debuffs are established here. Ordinary visible light is **not** automatically equivalent to a magical source of power; the distinction must be respected until clarified.

### Withering character and VFX direction — Proposed

Use several visually understandable **conceptual states**, not a fixed gameplay staging system:

- **Unaffected:** physically present, recognisable Arhîn body and normal material response.
- **Emotionally affected:** movement, pose, expression and behaviour become increasingly troubled or threatening; avoid implying a compulsory visible skin change at the first sign of distress.
- **Physically degrading:** skin opacity progressively lessens; transparent skin and layered internal silhouettes should communicate material disappearance, not merely desaturation or a generic undead appearance.
- **Fully Withered:** a barely corporeal, violent shadow. Readable gesture and silhouette matter more than particle density or graphic distortion.

VFX should feel uncanny and tragic rather than casually demonic. Whether a Withered shadow retains its identity, can recover or can be destroyed remains unresolved.

## 2.5 The Three-Caste Society — Established

Mainstream Arhîn society is organised into **three castes**, alongside a socially distinct outsider caste of naturalists (see below):

| Caste | Established social description | Spatial and art implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I — Intelligentsia** | Highest of the three castes | Power, education and privileged access to sunlight; monumental elevated spaces are particularly associated with the wealthy and powerful |
| **II — Craftsmen, traders and tutors** | Intermediate caste | Professional workshops, teaching and commercial functions; dependants of the caste above |
| **III — All others** | Lowest caste | Domestic, service and other unclassified work; dependants of the intermediate caste |

The **lower castes are invariably indentured servants of those in the caste above them**, living **under their protection** and **on the ground floors of their masters' abodes**.

The system is not simply a distinction of wealth: control of shelter and access to protection is woven into social dependence. Its architecture should depict the imbalance without portraying indenture as an uncomplicated luxury.

**Playable Arhîn characters may originate from any of the three mainstream castes. Caste is a matter of character background and setting lore, not a game-enforced character-creation choice or mechanical restriction.** Do not require the player to select a caste or enforce caste-based limitations on character creation, classes, equipment, progression or other gameplay. The society's in-world caste distinctions remain canon; their treatment in quests, dialogue and NPC interactions is a separate narrative design question, not an automatic caste gameplay system.

**Further established (§2.20):** caste normally persists by inheritance; extraordinary magical or martial achievement may secure rare elevation. The lower caste receives wages and may acquire personal wealth, but only the upper caste owns land. Middle-caste businesses operate under upper-caste patronage. Indentured servants can change households, though it is often considered an insult. Accommodation is guaranteed, but food is not uniformly guaranteed. Inter-caste marriage is possible though unusual. Detailed legal rights remain open.

### Vertical social organisation — Proposed spatial interpretation

- At the top: large luminous rooms, open views, glass walls, domes and private terraces where privileged inhabitants enjoy direct solar exposure.
- In the middle: workspaces, teaching halls, commercial galleries, stairs and controlled passageways.
- At the base: sheltered communal and service spaces, retained within the master's structure and receiving indirect light or supervised magical protection when necessary.

The ground floor is specifically established as the place where lower-caste dependants dwell; the above hierarchy describes an **art direction** for how such a building could communicate that fact, not a mandatory floor-by-floor legal plan.

**Critical tension:** protection may be genuine and necessary, but is also a form of power exercised over dependants. Residential design must convey both.

## 2.6 Government — Established

The Arhîn are ruled by **Queen Ilya**, who is also **High Priestess of Tiandre**.

Queen Ilya rules from the mystical **Suntower**, a structure so intensely bright that it **cannot be looked upon without protection**.

The game faction definition identifies the Arhîn dominion as **Culosimlond**, on the shores of the **Culosíma**, and describes the queen as reclusive. It also describes high nobles obeying her commands. The name **Culosimlond** is therefore an existing in-game faction name, not a newly devised term.

**Histalion** is the Arhîn **supreme military commander** and rules territory in the **Mortaure**. His command exists despite the Arhîn having **no standing military**. Defensive forces usually fight in their existing household/scholarly communities; foreign campaigns are organised centrally by Histalion.

**Established:** the immortal **Tinithas** have ruled their territories since appointment at the start of the Second Age for resisting Kabithh: **Úrien** (Daughter of Sunlight) rules **Arataure** from **Ara-Úr**; **Iluvantar** rules **Mortaure** from **Thel-Didnel**; **Histalion** rules **Histaure** from a city not yet named, and is supreme military commander. **Queen Ilya** replaced **Queen Ilvielle** and became High Priestess of Tiandre at the start of that Age. Precise constitutional distinctions remain open; do not impose Eldani hereditary-house structures.

## 2.7 Religion — Worship of the fey deities — Established / Proposed

### Pantheon and organised worship — Established

The Arhîn practise organised worship of various **fey deities**, with **Tiandre** as their principal deity. They have clergy. Worship of multiple deities is an established religious tradition, not merely informal reverence for magical beings.

The Twelve Grey Ones (six per Court) are **political** factions rather than opposed magical or moral essences. Arhîn worship Grey Ones from **both** Courts, although not all enjoy equal esteem.

| Summer Court | Established identity |
|---|---|
| **Tiandre** | Queen of the Summer Court; principal Arhîn deity, whose High Priestess is Queen Ilya |
| **Auris Koth** | Prince of Daylight; bestowed the Annokal, highly revered |
| **Othruum** | The Greenbringer; strongly associated with druidism |
| **Titramech** | The Seven-Legged Stalker; creator of Aurësén with Ninathell; worship cautiously regarded, not prohibited like Kabithh's |
| **Myrhufrun** | The Stag King; patron of the Cehîn at the Moonlight Well |
| **Lurur** | Maiden of the Three Tides |

| Winter Court | Established identity |
|---|---|
| **Ythralla** | Queen of the Winter Court; maintains the frozen Sanctum of Starlight and Auris Koth's body |
| **Kabithh** | Lady of Deceit, formerly Cenotet; enemy of Arhîn, worship heavily forbidden, patron of Durhîn |
| **Ninathell** | Princess of Glass; co-creator of Aurësén with Titramech |
| **Gov Govril** | The Lord of Bats |
| **Eelibron** | The Emerald Jester |
| **Unspecified** | Sixth Winter Court member not yet recalled |

The Grey Ones were once Malamer who entered the Grey with the Cabal of Twelve. All are still alive and active in the Ninth Age but trapped in the physical world with reduced power. The existence and social roles of any other religious beings are not inferred.

**Priesthoods are independently organised for each deity.** Queen Ilya is the highly respected High Priestess of Tiandre but **does not govern all priesthoods**. Clergy are **socially stratified**, with almost no mobility between strata.

**Over the Ninth Age, fey cults have risen among Arhîn who regard the Withering as a deserved consequence of the Aurësén's actions rather than solely an injustice.** These movements must be distinguished from the longstanding, organised worship of fey deities: the existence of the cults does not establish their precise relationship with recognised priesthoods or mean that all fey worship is cultic. **These cults have emerged in connection with deities of both the Summer and Winter Courts; they are not predominantly or exclusively a Winter Court phenomenon.** **The Arhîn response differs sharply by geography. Within the Great Forests, the emerging fey cults are tolerated but regarded with suspicion at best by the religious and secular establishment. Outside the Great Forests, Arhîn authorities deliberately allow the cults to proliferate.** Their permissiveness is **an instrument of foreign influence, not indifference**: the Arhîn use these cults to shape and define which religious and social practices are considered acceptable in neighbouring regions. **In some countries the cults also constitute an internal threat that keeps local military forces occupied, distracting those states from potential action against the Arhîn.** **Some foreign fey cults receive active Arhîn support, depending on their practices and intentions; others are not necessarily supported.** A specific example is found along **the border between Histaure and the Kingdom of Khalargat**, where the Arhîn use fey cults to **keep the Endar (hill dwarves) under control**. **Foreign fey cults have both forms of origin and leadership: some arise predominantly among the native inhabitants of neighbouring kingdoms and are influenced covertly by the Arhîn, while others are founded and led by Arhîn expatriates who attract local followers.** This is variation between cults, not a requirement that every cult share the same composition. **Supported cults are largely unaware that Arhîn authorities and agents are using their activities to advance Arhîn political interests.** Their leaders and members generally do not grasp the extent of this covert manipulation, although this does not preclude exceptions. **Neighbouring rulers, by contrast, are usually aware or strongly suspect that the Arhîn are interfering through these cults, but generally lack sufficient proof or the means to prevent the interference effectively.** **Two pressures constrain suppression at once: fear that direct action against Arhîn-backed cults could provoke the Arhîn's formidable magical power, and the cults' deep roots in local communities and political institutions, which make crackdowns liable to trigger widespread domestic unrest.** **This foreign-cult strategy is largely concealed from ordinary Arhîn, who generally do not know that influential nobles exploit these movements to affect neighbouring kingdoms.** The manipulation of foreign fey cults is generally orchestrated by the Arhîn nobility, not centrally directed by Queen Ilya. **Queen Ilya is aware of these activities but deliberately chooses not to intervene; she does not personally organise or direct them.** **Noble households normally pursue such operations independently, including backing competing cults that may work against other houses' interests. A superior household can demand that subordinate households coordinate or align their activities instead.** Knowledge is concentrated among the nobles and other political or religious insiders involved in or aware of these activities; the specific hierarchy between households and the extent of coordination imposed in particular cases remain undefined. This contrasts with the cultists' limited awareness and foreign governments' suspicions; neither every Arhîn elite nor every foreign ruler necessarily knows the full scope of the activity. The relative weight of these pressures varies by kingdom and has not been established. The precise kinds of support and mechanisms of control are not yet established, nor are the cults' specific divine affiliations and practices. The existing prohibition on Kabithh's worship is not implicitly rescinded.

**Annokal:** Auris Koth granted it after the Black Winter, when Kabithh had used Titramech's blood to control the Aurësén. It prevented that control but granted neither immortality nor magic. Auris Koth's incapacitation during the Serpent War caused its failure, and **restoring him would reactivate it and end the Withering**. Queen Ilya and the Tinithas do not know he can be restored; see [World Lore and History](../World-Lore-and-History-PDD.md).

### Distinction between fey worship and druidism — Established / Proposed

**Established:** fey worship has a druidic element, but organised Arhîn religion and druidism are distinct traditions. Druidism is often expressed through the worship and teachings of **Othruum, the Greenbringer**. Do not equate all fey worship with druidism, all Arhîn clergy with druids, or the use of woodland shrines with a specifically druidic practice.

Tiandre remains the principal deity of the Arhîn; Auris Koth remains highly revered for bestowing the Gift of Light. **Othruum is a deity of the Summer Court.** His association with druidism does not establish his relative prominence among the Arhîn, his relationship to the Grey Ones, or that all druids worship him exclusively. The precise teachings, domains, symbols and druidic practices remain to be defined. Do not assume druidism is restricted to the Arhîn.

**Proposed visual distinction:** organised Arhîn clergy retain their refined cultural tailoring, ceremonial textiles and carefully crafted shrine objects. Druidic attire and ritual objects may place greater emphasis on practical woodland clothing and natural materials, but specific druidic iconography, sacred locations, vestments and rites require separate approval. Woodland setting alone must not be used to identify a character or shrine as druidic. In particular, Arhîn shrines dedicated to Othruum use the same sacred-site vocabulary as shrines of other deities; do not assign them a separate druidic grove kit. Avoid default antlers, animal pelts, leaf-covered robes or living-wood accessories on ordinary Arhîn priests.

### Arhîn naturalists — Established

Arhîn naturalists occupy a socially distinct, effectively separate caste of hermits and outsiders, rather than a normal profession within the mainstream three-caste hierarchy. They possess deep knowledge of the natural world. Through that pursuit they become increasingly attuned to the darker side of Arhîn and Aurësén ancestry. They are regarded with suspicion and fear within Arhîn society and are characterised as **druidic occultists**.

Their visual identity should reflect their separation from ordinary Arhîn clergy and lay society: animal hides, antlers and other animal parts are established attire and adornment directions. The druidic practice **unintentionally** deepens Arhîn attunement to their darker Aurësén ancestry; Eldani naturalists **do not** undergo this phenomenon. Proposed treatments include weathered leather, bone or teeth accessories, practical layered garments, subdued earth colours and selective remnants of recognisable Arhîn tailoring. These materials need not imply that every naturalist wears identical equipment or that every item is a ritual object. Do not equate their ancestral attunement with the Withering, evil, specific magical powers or worship of a particular deity without further lore. Their social and legal position relative to the three mainstream castes, particular occult practices and relationship with Othruum's priesthood remain open.

### Woodland shrines — Established

The Arhîn **do not dedicate large temples to their deities**. They pay tribute to them at **small, sacred shrines in woodland areas**. Organised religion and the existence of clergy do not imply large urban temples, cathedrals or monumental religious precincts. Queen Ilya's position as High Priestess does not by itself make the Suntower a temple.

**An Arhîn grove dedicated to Othruum is identical in kind to a sacred shrine dedicated to any other deity.** It belongs to the same Arhîn religious tradition, with no special druidic architectural or institutional category. Worship of Othruum at an Arhîn shrine does not by itself make its worshippers or officiants druids. Differences in deity-specific iconography or rites remain to be established and must not be used to invent a separate shrine type for Othruum.

The specific distribution of shrines, their custodianship, access, rites, offerings, frequency of worship and whether shrines are shared by multiple deities remain unresolved.

### Shrine art direction — Proposed

Use the existing Arhîn design language at an intimate scale: finely shaped pale stone, graceful curved supports, restrained bronze detailing and preserved older Aurësén craftsmanship where appropriate. Woodland surroundings are integral to the shrine's identity; the built element should remain subordinate in scale to the natural site.

- Modest freestanding altars, low stone platforms, carved markers and small open-sided shrine structures may provide modular starting points.
- Selective canopy openings, filtered daylight or small clearings may connect shrine composition to the Arhîn relationship with light; do not assume the structures provide protection from the Withering.
- Tiandre-associated shrines may use warmer material tones, subtle seasonal ornament and controlled shafts of light.
- Auris Koth-associated shrines may explore daylight-oriented composition and early Aurësén-derived details.
- Deity-specific emblems, statues, offerings, carved figures and magical effects are not established. Woodland shrines are not inherently druidic sites and must not be assigned Othruum's symbolism merely because they are surrounded by trees. Do not invent a universal sun emblem or import Eldani religious symbols.
- Avoid grand temple buildings, oversized altars, sprawling paved courtyards, obligatory treehouse architecture and excessive magical emission.

### Clergy and religious dress — Established / Proposed

**Established:** the Arhîn have clergy, and Queen Ilya is High Priestess of Tiandre. The organisation of the principal deity's clergy, separate priesthoods for other deities, ranks, caste status, duties, training, residency and relationship to royal authority remain to be defined.

**Proposed art direction:** religious clothing should retain distinctively Arhîn tailoring, refined collars, geometric shoulder treatments and elongated cloth panels, adapted to movement through woodland and attendance at small shrines. Use restrained textiles, metal fastenings and deity-specific accents only once approved. Do not copy Eldani clerical hoods, masks, stoles or ecclesiastical heraldry as default Arhîn dress. Avoid treating all priests as members of the highest caste, immensely wealthy, or inherently combat-capable. Queen Ilya's exceptional status should not be used as a baseline for ordinary clergy attire. Ordinary Arhîn clergy must remain visually and conceptually distinct from druids; shared woodland associations do not imply identical vestments or practices.

**Do not assume** that Tiandre directly grants immunity to the Withering, that the Suntower is powered by Tiandre, or that devotion is required to control magic. Those relationships are open.

## 2.8 The Suntower — Established / Proposed

**Established:** The Suntower is Queen Ilya's seat and is **blindingly bright**, such that it cannot be safely looked upon without protection.

**Proposed landmark design:**
- a very tall, unmistakable vertical silhouette rising above the surrounding canopy and ordinary Arhîn structures;
- a monumental domed or faceted summit, supported by a physically convincing structural framework;
- luminous glass and reflective or refractive architectural elements, with a strong single light source or concentrated glow rather than arbitrary emission across every surface;
- elevated terraces and long vertical supports that convey royal distance from the world below;
- surrounding architecture whose sightlines and shading acknowledge the tower's overwhelming brilliance.

The Suntower's supernatural visual effect should be presented with a scalable combination of material emission, local lighting, atmospheric scattering and restrained camera effects. The camera must remain playable; the fiction of impossible direct sight should not automatically require permanently obscuring the game view.

**Unresolved:** exact shape, source of radiance, whether it is the tallest building, material composition, the nature of its protective viewing devices, and the relation between its brightness and the Withering.

## 2.9 Geography — The Three Great Forests — Established

The Arhîn primarily inhabit three Great Forests around the **Culosíma**:

| Forest | Established meaning |
|---|---|
| **Arataure** | **Day Forest** |
| **Mortaure** | **Dusk Forest** |
| **Histaure** | **Dawn Forest** |

These are names and cultural regions; they do **not** necessarily imply permanent day, dusk or dawn lighting cycles.

### Regional visual differentiation — Proposed

**Arataure — Day Forest:** favour open, high canopy spaces, bright sun shafts, pale masonry, clear glass and warm golden-metal detailing. Towers can appear confidently sun-facing.

**Mortaure — Dusk Forest:** favour denser shade, copper or amber glazing, contrasting pockets of magic-sourced light and more visible tension between shelter and exposure.

**Histaure — Dawn Forest:** favour mist-filtered light, cool pale stone, opalescent glass and delicate luminous accents amid dense greenery.

These differences are provisional environmental motifs, not claims about biome, chronology, magical mechanics or terrain maps. All three should retain the same Arhîn construction vocabulary.

### Forest integration — Proposed

Tower crowns and domes should emerge **above or through the canopy**, where exposure is better. Stable masonry or other engineered foundations, supported terraces, narrow vertical building profiles and elevated connections make the architecture plausible in a forest environment.

Do not turn Arhîn settlements into a uniform network of treehouses. Large trunks, roots and living vegetation may shape streets and sightlines, but the architectural identity is **light-seeking towers**, not trees as buildings.

Controlled canopy gaps, courtyards and shafts of light can tell a social and ecological story without implying that every tree is magically altered.

## 2.10 Yer-Aranie — Established / Proposed

**Established:** **Yer-Aranie** was a great Aurësén city, inhabited before its destruction in the catastrophic conclusion of the Serpent War.

**Established comparative constraint:** Aurësén civilisation reached an architectural peak that present Arhîn builders generally cannot match; the loss of many original master craftsmen and designers, both in the Serpent War and subsequently to the Withering, must remain apparent in how the ages are compared.

**Proposed art direction:** its ruins should feel **older and architecturally distinct** from contemporary Arhîn tower settlements. They may contain monumental public plazas, broken light-gathering structures, ruined transparent enclosures and remnants of an unusually confident civilisation from before the Gift was lost.

The contrast should be archaeological rather than a simple colour swap. The Aurësén were the ancestors of the Arhîn, but their built world need not have been organised around the same desperate need for sunlight.

**Unresolved:** the city's original plan, geography, size, surviving materials, precise destruction mechanism and whether its ruins remain accessible. No assertion of floating buildings, technological superiority or a specific magical cause of destruction is implied.

## 2.11 Architectural Design Language — Proposed

**Central architectural principle:** **light is both a survival resource and a visible instrument of social hierarchy**.

The wealthiest and most powerful Arhîn **live in high towers with glass walls and domed roofs** to maximise exposure to sunlight. This is established and must anchor the entire asset language.

Suggested supporting design grammar:

| Asset family | Visual direction |
|---|---|
| Privileged residences | Slender, tall towers with glass-wall panels, domed roofs and light-facing chambers; **genuinely opulent architectural treatment is restricted to the highest rulers**, not every wealthy inhabitant |
| Structural frames | Elegant but functional stone, metal or composite ribs and mullions; legible beams, joints and supports |
| Shared residential levels | Low, robust foundations; service halls, sheltered galleries and domestic rooms at ground level |
| Work and teaching spaces | Compact workshops, arcaded galleries, educational halls and trade rooms attached to larger residences |
| Civic buildings | Taller ceremonial halls, luminous domes, stairs, vaults and colonnades; not large temples dedicated to the fey deities |
| Woodland shrines | Small sacred structures integrated into woodland, with intimate scale and Arhîn-derived construction; exact forms and deity-specific ornament provisional |
| Forest infrastructure | Raised connections, bridge sections, retaining structures, clearings, stair towers and sheltered ground paths |
| Defensive elements | Defensive outer shells, overlapping sloped bastions, strongly controlled gates and protected open-to-sun inner spaces, with glazing concentrated away from directly vulnerable outer faces |
| Ancient Aurësén ruins | Monumental remains with related but earlier shape language, distinct from survival-driven modern towers |

### A millennium of diminished craftsmanship — Established and proposed visual translation

**Established:** Arhîn society is living through a long architectural decline. Important Aurësén designers and craftsmen were killed during the Serpent War or subsequently lost to the Withering. Therefore even splendid contemporary construction should be recognisably **less ambitious and less accomplished than the greatest Aurësén work**. Only the highest rulers still possess truly opulent residences.

**Approved cultural art-direction implication:** do not make all Arhîn buildings pristine, extravagantly glazed palaces. A typical tower may remain elegant and expensive by Eldani standards, but its construction should show simplified profiles, repeated stock components, imperfect replacements, restrained ornament and older exceptional elements selectively preserved within later work. A very few royal or supreme-ruler precincts may retain outstanding luxury.

**Proposed visual methods:** a broken rhythm of replaced dome panes; a finely carved ancient colonnade connected to a comparatively plain later gallery; patched metal mullions; different masonry epochs; abandoned ornamental recesses now filled with practical stone; smaller and more repetitive modern arches set beside far finer surviving Aurësén details. Such contrasts should emerge from coherent construction history, not random damage decals.

Decline is **not** general collapse, technological primitiveness or universal poverty. The Arhîn are still sophisticated, intensely magical and capable of maintaining formidable defences and their defining glazed towers.

### Geometry

Preferred silhouettes: elongated vertical stems, restrained tapering, large domes or lantern-like crowns, gently segmented curved elements and recognisable translucent wall areas. Broad flat glass skyscraper panels and science-fiction curtain-wall grids are not the intended character.

The structure beneath glass must have an intelligible load path. Larger glass fields should have appropriately sized frames and repeatable panel dimensions. Magical reinforcement may be depicted only as a proposal until its existence and form are established.

### Contrast with Eldani architecture

The Eldani emphasise masonry mass, defensible enclosures, steep rooflines, heraldic fortifications and historical accretion. The Arhîn should emphasise slender **verticality, glazing, domes, filtered light and dependence on controlled access**. Their cities must be recognisable by silhouette even when stripped of banners or surface ornament.

### Light and caste

The brightest and most private spaces belong to the most privileged. The deeper living levels should reveal the need for magical support where direct sun is scarce. This contrast is crucial; it should not be flattened by making every interior equally luminous.

Magical illumination can be visualised through controlled light wells, enclosed luminaires, protected chambers or conduits, **but the devices and their energy systems remain proposals**. A normal lantern must not silently become an approved replacement for sunlight or magical sources of power.

### Fortress archetype: the Sunward Redoubt — Approved concept direction

**Status:** approved **cultural fortress concept**, not a claim that every Arhîn fortress has this exact plan, name, origin, size or magical mechanism.

The fortress has a deliberately defensive silhouette unlike an Eldani castle or an uninterrupted Aurësén pleasure-palace: **a heavy protective outer shell wrapped around a delicate, light-filled architectural heart**.

- **Petal-shaped defensive shell:** massive, sloping, overlapping stone bastions arranged radially around the stronghold. Interlocking faces and flanking approaches make the perimeter read as a functional defensive work rather than an ornamental wall. Number of bastions and exact military engineering remain open.
- **Defended open-air solar court:** a substantial sun-exposed inner courtyard protected by surrounding walls and gateways. Maintaining access to daylight is a guiding architectural objective even in wartime.
- **One exceptional ancient core:** a distinguished **glazed, domed central tower**, visually representing surviving greater Aurësén workmanship. This is a *concept choice for this example fortress*, not a universal rule that all contemporary fortresses contain an Aurësén tower.
- **Sun-catching reflective arrays:** large, visibly angled bronze or other reflective panels suggested for redirecting sunlight into the fortress's sheltered spaces; they must have readable supports and adjustable or plausible fixed orientations. Their actual biological efficacy, whether magical assistance is needed and the underlying technology are **not established**.
- **Diminished contemporary outer construction:** later bastions, galleries and service ranges use larger, plainer pieces, reused elements, replaced glazing and conspicuous but purposeful repairs. The outer fortress should communicate a civilisation maintaining inherited magnificence rather than duplicating its ancestral peak.
- **Vertical caste contrast:** prestigious sunlit rooms and outlooks occupy the upper central precinct, while practical defence, workshops and dependent residences occupy less luxurious lower levels. Ground-floor habitation by indentured dependants follows the established caste structure.
- **Site relationship:** an elevated forest-edge or rocky approach is a plausible setting for this fortress **concept**, but cliffs, waterfalls and other incidental features in exploratory illustrations are not required cultural canon.

**Explicit exclusions:** a sprawling open palace complex; a uniform skyline of equally sumptuous glass towers; unprotected perimeter walls built primarily of fragile curtain glazing; arbitrary floating architecture; default sunburst flags, purple livery or religious emblems copied from exploratory imagery without separate heraldic approval.

The image-concept composition and any presentation-board framing are **not** approved world assets. Actual game-asset references must follow the [Graphical Approach PDD](../../Graphical-Approach-PDD.md): one isolated complete three-quarter-view modelling reference against a plain neutral background unless another format is expressly requested.

## 2.13 Heraldry and Ornament — Established / Proposed

**Established:** No specific Arhîn royal banner, caste device, state insignia, symbol of Tiandre or icon for any Grey One is defined by the present brief.

**Proposed motif families:**
- repeated curved dome ribs, solar arcs and refracted light patterns;
- restrained concentric and radial geometry;
- architectural tracery echoing framed glass panels;
- formal representations of seasonal light and courts.

Symbols of Tiandre, Queen Ilya, Culosimlond and individual caste institutions should be treated as **distinct, unresolved** identities. Their art must not invent a single mandatory sun emblem and assign it to all of them.

The name **Grey Ones** does not itself imply that their symbols, bodies or architecture are grey.

## 2.14 Clothing and Physical Presentation — Proposed

**Established appearance:** slender and graceful; subtly elongated skulls tapering posteriorly, short pointed ears set high and close to the head, prominent brows and cheekbones, strong sharp jawlines and slightly projecting pointed chins, pointed noses; dull burnished-gold, blue or green eyes without natural glow. Childhood/adolescence lasts **four to five years longer than in humans**. Mature adults acquire faint wrinkles slowly; injuries and an air of wisdom communicate age more readily. Exact skin/hair palettes and final implementation proportions remain open.

Potential costume language:
- **Intelligentsia / powerful:** long, carefully cut layered garments, light-catching surfaces, translucent over-layers, refined metal ornaments and formal silhouettes that prioritise upper-body visibility in lit spaces.
- **Craftspeople, traders and tutors:** precise, practical tailoring; tool and trade identifiers; hard-wearing fabrics suitable for workshops, commerce and instruction.
- **All others:** durable, repaired and less ornamented workwear appropriate to ground-level domestic and service life.

Caste distinction should be readable through silhouette, quality, cut, accessories and environmental context, not merely three fixed colours. Indenture is a social structure, not a reason to make every lower-caste character visually identical.

**Proposed exceptional military equipment** may use slender but plausible protection, curved or finely proportioned weapon forms, glass-derived motifs in ornament and compact symbolic devices. Such armoured figures should not be interpreted as evidence of large permanent infantry formations. Arhîn martial visual identity should place **magical capability and carefully conserved exceptional equipment** before generic rows of melee footsoldiers. Avoid compulsory glowing crystals, nonfunctional oversized armour or claims that every Arhîn is an accomplished sorcerer. Almost all Arhîn warriors, however, possess **some magical ability**, even if primarily martial.

## 2.15 Magic and Everyday Life — Established / Proposed

**Established:** Arhîn society is intensely magical; magical sources of power can protect against withdrawal that would otherwise cause the Withering. The Arhîn do not naturally possess godlike powers.

**Proposed environmental storytelling:**
- public runic scriptures and apparently motionless Arhîn viewers, providing recognisable daily-life scenes of memory contemplation;
- supervised rooms, wells or fixtures that visibly provide magical refuge where sunlight cannot reach;
- upper-caste-controlled access to particular bright or magically protected areas;
- workshops and scholarly halls with precise, purposeful magical equipment;
- sheltered movement routes that make long residence in deep forest environments socially and logistically plausible.

Do not assume constant free energy, universal magical illumination, flying towers, unlimited teleportation, immortality via resurrection or any defined combat ability. These belong to other design systems and may require later lore decisions.

## 2.16 Historical Layering — Established history / proposed visual translation

Representative sites may distinguish:
1. **Aurësén inheritance:** the surviving works and remnants of an older architectural peak, potentially exhibiting craftsmanship no longer reproducible by most current builders;
2. **The Serpent War's aftermath:** destruction, depopulation and loss of master artisans and designers, with further loss across centuries to the Withering;
3. **The current Arhîn adaptation:** architecture reorganised to favour sunlight and protected magical access, with reduced technical ambition and greater reliance on simpler, reusable building parts;
4. **Present-day selective splendour and maintenance:** repaired glazing, replaced ribs, mismatched masonry, adapted older structures, dependent ground-floor residences, and exceptional opulence retained only by the highest rulers.

These are visual strata, not a formally approved chronology, and not all settlements need contain every layer.

## 2.17 Production and Reuse Constraints

Arhîn components must follow the [Graphical Approach PDD](../../Graphical-Approach-PDD.md): material approval first, **atomic modular architectural parts**, meaningful medium-scale geometry, reusable textures and masks, controlled runtime weathering, scalable LODs and gameplay-distance validation.

The glass-heavy identity introduces additional constraints:
- Prefer an intelligible set of reusable glass-panel dimensions, mullions, ribs, dome segments, cornices and supporting walls.
- Large glass walls and dome shapes must read in silhouette without requiring every panel to be fully transparent.
- URP transparency, double-sided surfaces, sorting, overdraw and reflections must be budgeted; use opaque or selectively translucent treatments when the appearance allows.
- Material emission should not substitute for actual playable illumination or guarantee high exposure for lower-caste residents.
- Weathering, grime, moss and ageing must be controlled through reusable materials and shader profiles, not unique geometry per building.
- Distinct regional and caste variations should primarily reuse approved construction kits and change material treatment, detailing and arrangement.

## 2.18 Arhîn Art Acceptance Criteria

An Arhîn concept, building or scene should:
1. Read as Arhîn from its **tall glazed, domed construction and light-seeking silhouette**, not simply from leaf motifs or glowing ornaments.
2. Express the relationship between **upper-level privilege** and **ground-floor dependence**.
3. Convey a magical but physically grounded civilisation that can plausibly exist within great forests.
4. Preserve the distinction between sunlight, magical power and decorative lights.
5. Show the Withering as progressively corporeal disappearance, not default dark-elf or undead makeup.
6. Keep Queen Ilya, Tiandre, Auris Koth, the Grey Ones and the Suntower visually meaningful without inventing undocumented religious symbols. Portray organised fey worship through small sacred woodland shrines and clergy rather than large deity temples; do not conflate royal architecture with religious sites.
7. Remain distinct from Eldani masonry-and-heraldry architecture.
8. Meet grounded mid-poly, modularity, material and performance requirements at MMO camera distances.
9. Allow Arataure, Mortaure and Histaure variants to appear related without relying on arbitrary permanent times of day.
10. Represent public runic scripture viewing as a commonplace, inward-looking practice: Arhîn viewers may seem simply to stare at patterns to outsiders.
11. Contemporary grandeur should read as **splendour in decline** relative to Aurësén excellence, with true opulence restricted to the highest rulers.
12. A Sunward Redoubt-style fortress should read as a **defensible shell around a protected sunlit court**, retaining one exceptional ancient dome while showing simpler repaired later construction.
13. Military scenes must **not imply a standing conventional army**; their strongest Arhîn identity should be intense magical capability, rare preserved expertise and pre-emptive diplomacy or intelligence.
14. A military scene should primarily feature **small battlemage groups, on foot or using forest-mobile salamander-like mounts**, and should avoid massed open-field formations as the normal Arhîn or historic Aurësén method.

## 2.19 Military, Diplomacy and Deterrence — Established / Proposed

### The Arhîn approach to security — Established

- The Arhîn **do not possess a standing military**. Earlier exploratory proposals for standing professional infantry households, permanent garrison armies or a conventional large heavy-infantry backbone are **superseded**, not approved lore.
- They prefer to **influence world events through espionage and diplomacy**, seeking to ensure their enemies have neither the desire nor opportunity to attack them.
- **Fey cults are also instruments of Arhîn foreign influence.** Outside the Great Forests, influential Arhîn nobles exploit and selectively support their proliferation in order to influence acceptable religious and social practices among neighbouring peoples. **Queen Ilya knows that the nobility manipulate foreign cults and chooses not to intervene, but does not herself direct the operations; these are generally noble-led undertakings.** **Individual noble households generally operate independently and may support rival cults, unless a superior household requires coordinated action.** In some neighbouring countries, the resulting domestic threat occupies military forces and distracts those states from confronting the Arhîn. **Arhîn support is selective, depending on a cult's practices and aims**, rather than universally extended to every foreign fey cult. **Cults operating along the Histaure–Kingdom of Khalargat border are used by the Arhîn to keep the Endar (hill dwarves) under control.** **The cults themselves are generally unaware of the extent of Arhîn manipulation and of the political purposes their activities serve.** The exact resources, methods, agents and extent of direct control are unestablished.
- Despite the decline of their civilisation relative to the Aurësén peak, the Arhîn remain **extremely formidable**.
- The defining basis of their formidable power is their **intensely magical nature**. Arhîn defence and warfare must not be primarily represented by conventional melee footsoldiers.
- **Histalion** holds the position of **supreme military commander** and **rules territory in the Mortaure**.
- The existence of a supreme military commander **does not** imply an always-mobilised army.
- The enormous scale and difficult terrain of the **Great Forests** make the movement of conventional armies difficult.
- Arhîn military strength is primarily exercised by **small groups of battlemages**, some fighting on foot and others **mounted on salamander-like creatures adapted to moving easily through forests**.
- The Arhîn **rarely engage in open battle** because they lack the numbers available to many of their enemies.
- **The Aurësén likewise faced numerical inferiority** and rarely met their enemies in open battle. This is a continuity of military circumstance across the Serpent War, not merely a contemporary adaptation to decline.

These principles override any inference from the Sunward Redoubt fortress concept that the Arhîn routinely keep permanent conventional garrisons. The existence of fortifications does not establish permanent troops stationed in them.

### Cultural and strategic interpretation — Proposed

The Arhîn should generally seek **victory before battle**: discouraging enemies through diplomacy, intelligence collection, careful influence over foreign interests and the credible prospect of severe magical retaliation. Their reluctance to wage conventional war must **not** be confused with inability to fight.

Where violence cannot be prevented, the Arhîn's primary military instrument is **the small battlemage group**, operating **on foot or mounted on forest-adapted salamander-like creatures**. The strategic challenge of marching armies through the Great Forests, combined with Arhîn numerical inferiority, favours dispersed movement and actions that do not require a massed battle line. The exact magical effects, numbers, unit command structures and non-magical support elements remain to be defined; no system of teleportation, magical siege engines, universal warding or particular offensive spells is established here.

Arhîn longevity, historical memory and deep magical culture may inform strategic caution and institutional continuity, but must not be used as a basis for declaring that every individual Arhîn is a powerful mage. Their dependence on sunlight or magical sources of power remains a relevant strategic vulnerability whose exact operational consequences are unresolved.

### Battlemage groups and forest-mounted warfare — Established foundations / proposed doctrine

**Established deployment form:** Arhîn combat power is primarily concentrated in **groups of battlemages**, both **unmounted** and **mounted on a salamander-like forest-traversing creature**. This creature is a military mount adapted to moving easily through forest terrain. Its name, natural history, size, temperament, anatomy beyond the broad salamander-like description, magical properties and precise means of traversal remain undecided.

**Established strategic constraint:** The Great Forests are large and difficult to cross with conventional armies. Arhîn numbers are insufficient to regularly meet their enemies in conventional open battle. **The Aurësén faced the same numerical asymmetry and also rarely fought open battles**; this feature of their military tradition therefore predates the Serpent War.

**Proposed implications, not additional canon:**
- On-foot battlemage teams can operate in locations where mounts are inappropriate, including defensive positions and restricted routes.
- Mounted battlemage teams could use the salamander-like creature's forest mobility for rapid movement between dispersed objectives without depending upon roads suited to large infantry formations.
- Intelligence-led selection of engagements, local concentration of magical force and withdrawal before an enemy can assemble superior numbers are plausible doctrinal developments.
- Prepared refuges and protection from Withering may become important during prolonged operations, but their construction, portability, spell requirements and logistics have not been established.
- Forest mobility does not prove that the mount climbs vertical trunks, traverses walls, jumps extraordinary distances or uses supernatural transport. Its actual capabilities require a dedicated creature design.

The battlemage is the **defining martial archetype** for Arhîn concept work, while conventional plate-armoured footsoldiers are occasional secondary or exceptional figures rather than the standard military force. No standing army should be inferred from the existence of organised battlemage groups; their mobilisation and peacetime organisation are unresolved.

### Histalion — Established scope and open boundaries

Histalion is **the supreme military commander** and a **territorial ruler in the Mortaure**. His title, personal magical capabilities, relation to Queen Ilya, specific territory, political alliances, peacetime responsibilities and authority to mobilise Arhîn society have **not** been specified.

His office may be expressed through a distinctive residence, retinue or command venue in future art direction, but such representations are proposals pending additional cultural decisions.

### Military visual identity — Proposed

- Prioritise **small on-foot battlemage teams and mounted battlemages on forest-adapted salamander-like creatures**, depicting focused magical mastery, authority, inherited scholarship and carefully maintained operational equipment.
- Reserve heavy armour for appropriately justified exceptional defenders and rare circumstances, not the default visual population of an Arhîn settlement.
- Visualise espionage and diplomatic influence through environments, institutions, observers and discreet equipment where appropriate; avoid inventing a standard spy uniform.
- Depict military artefacts as inherited, preserved or comparatively simplified reproductions of Aurësén originals, consistent with the millennium of lost craftsmanship.
- Avoid defaulting to generic elven archers, permanent battalions, ranks of polearm infantry or huge armies simply because the Arhîn are formidable.
- Mount design should communicate **forest mobility and a salamander-like body plan**, not generic horses, dragons, giant war-lizards covered in armour or an implied supernatural movement ability.

**Hierarchy of emphasis:** diplomacy and espionage in peacetime; extraordinary magical deterrence; magical intervention if conflict occurs; conventional weapons and armour as supporting, not defining elements.

## 2.20 Arhîn Society and Institutions — Established Canon

### Caste, property and household duties

Mainstream Arhîn have **three castes**, usually inherited. **Elevation for exceptional magical ability, scholarly achievement or military achievement is possible**, but extraordinarily uncommon and celebrated by family, friends and neighbours. Wealth and caste are independent: even a **very wealthy** lower-caste person remains lower-caste.

**Only the upper caste can own land.** Middle-caste trade and business operate **under an upper-caste household's patronage**. Indentured dependants **receive wages**, can accumulate personal wealth and have **guaranteed accommodation on lower floors** of the house they serve. They **may leave for another household**, though this is often felt as an insult. Middle-caste households frequently **feed** their lower-caste dependants and **share meals as equals**. The **upper caste** offers **no guarantee of sustenance outside crises**. This is paid, mobile indenture, not a presumption of chattel slavery or unpaid lifetime confinement.

**Inter-caste marriage is permitted but considered odd**. Children usually receive the **higher caste** of their two parents; the higher-caste member has **no obligation to acknowledge an elevation in the status of the lower-caste spouse**.

Naturalists are a **socially separate outsider caste** of hermits and feared druidic occultists rather than a fourth conventional economic tier. Their deep naturalistic practices **unintentionally** attune them to the sinister side of Aurësén heritage, and their attire uses **animal hides, antlers and animal parts**. Comparable Eldani practices do not cause this effect.

### Reproduction, ageing and mixed ancestry

Arhîn are **naturally less fertile** than humans, but their extreme rarity of births has a concealed cause: a particular **berry used widely in the Great Forests' cuisine** acts as an **Arhîn contraceptive**. The Arhîn **do not know this**. Wild animals consume the same berry. When wild-animal populations boom, competition for the berry reduces Arhîn consumption, so **their birth rate increases in the same year**. The Arhîn instead believe those years reflect a shared gift of fertility for every creature in that region.

Arhîn abroad for extended periods are **more fertile** because they do not consume the berry regularly, with a **grace period after returning**. Distant warfare therefore commonly yields **a modest postwar increase in Arhîn births**. No berry name, dosage, biological thresholds or duration are established.

Childhood and adolescence last about **four to five years longer than for humans**. Thereafter ageing continues extremely slowly: **fine wrinkles** are minor; accumulated **injuries** and perceived **wisdom** give more obvious signs of age.

Within the Great Forests, interracial relationships are **socially unacceptable**; outside, they are **uncommon**. Aurësén and descendants are compatible **only with humans and orcs**, due to a **vague shared Malamer lineage**. Mixed children are **neither immortal nor subject to the Withering**, but their proportion of Aurësén ancestry can influence **emotional volatility** and **higher intelligence**. The exact hereditary thresholds remain open.

### Education and scholarly communities

All three castes receive **magical education**. Young Arhîn are initially taught by **dedicated early-years tutors** owing to the low birth rate. By adolescence, aptitude determines whether further sorcerer training is worthwhile. Promising pupils enter an **apprenticeship under an accomplished sorcerer**, alongside **approximately four to six other adolescent students**. Being apprenticed does not automatically confer upper-caste status; **extraordinary achievement** might later secure that elevation.

Mature sorcerers often join the service of **lords and ladies**, forming **small close-knit scholarly communities** responsible for **libraries** and the **magic of nearby towns**. Broad ability across **several disciplines** is expected for noble service, though individual **specialisations** naturally arise.

Those not selected for sorcery are **encouraged to train for combat**. They can learn within households or at the **few specialised martial schools**. Enrolment in a martial school effectively prevents a future **scholarly career**. Almost no Arhîn soldiers are completely nonmagical: even primarily weapon-using warriors possess **some ability in magic**.

### Military mobilisation

The Arhîn **do not maintain a standing army**. Sorcerers are **obliged to serve in wartime**. In a **defensive war**, combatants normally retain their established household and scholarly communities as units. For **campaigns abroad**, **Histalion** undertakes a more centralised military organisation. Smaller magically competent forces, on foot and on forest-adapted salamander-like mounts, remain their defining approach. **Exceptional military deeds** can secure a rare rise in caste.

### The Tinithas and authority

**Queen Ilya** replaced **Queen Ilvielle** and became **High Priestess of Tiandre** at the **start of the Second Age**. For resisting Kabithh, the three **immortal Tinithas** were granted territorial authority at that time; they **have held it ever since**:

| Region | Ruler | City |
|---|---|---|
| **Arataure** | **Lady Úrien**, Daughter of Sunlight | **Ara-Úr** |
| **Mortaure** | **Lord Iluvantar** | **Thel-Didnel** |
| **Histaure** | **Lord Histalion**, supreme military commander | **Undecided** |

Before the Terhat-Ilu, the Tinithas **deliberately allowed the Night Serpent to bind the world in its mind for a 1,000-year search for the Silver Key**. They then **claimed publicly that they had bound the Serpent beneath the Culosíma**. They believe the Ertniiagra's sacrifices help maintain the world's binding. In reality **Kabithh uses sylvan blood to suppress the Serpent itself**. The Tinithas do not know this; **Iluvantar suspects** Kabithh's manipulation because the bargain was her idea.

The Arhîn opposed **Achov III's exclusion and planned eviction of western centaurs** from their sacred migration route to the Moonlight Well, and sought to **remove Achov** to restore that migration. The Arhîn did **not know** that **Talen Perydain** had killed Achov's father and blamed the centaurs. Their intervention was independent of Perydain's conspiracy.

### Afterlife beliefs and funerals

The Arhîn **traditionally believe** that at death the soul would travel through the Moonlight Well to the **Grey**, to be reincarnated by a Grey One. **They now know the Well was never a gateway**, believe that the Grey is **inaccessible** and acknowledge that **they have no right to enter the Grey Ones' realm**. Do not describe the reincarnation belief as confirmed cosmological fact.

Funerals employ **ritual desiccation**. The wrapped body is **dehydrated** before the funeral proper. During the ceremony the extracted water is **poured into a river flowing to the Culosíma**. The wrapped, desiccated body is **cremated** and the ashes **scattered at a woodland shrine to the Grey One chosen by the deceased**. Those who have **fully Withered** have no body for the rite and are believed **spiritually lost without redemption**.

All Grey Ones' priesthoods are **independently organised** and **stratified**. Queen Ilya is highly revered among them, but is not their universal supreme priestess. **Kabithh's worship is forbidden**; **Titramech's** receives cautious, suspicious toleration.

### Contemporary knowledge of the Annokal

In the Sanctum of Starlight at Yer-Aranie, **Ythralla** holds the ice to preserve Auris Koth's body; she knows his soul is in a ring elsewhere. Restoration of the spirit to his body by magic **would restore the Annokal and stop the Withering**. Queen Ilya and the Tinithas **believe him permanently lost**. Kabithh mistakenly believes his spirit remains in the ice. See [World Lore and History](../World-Lore-and-History-PDD.md) for the bow, ring, Messa, Czera and Kabithh's intended second Black Winter.

### Neighbours of Aurësén descent

The **Durhîn**, Kabithh-serving Aurësén descendants who partially embraced the Withering, retain bodies but suffer persistent negative violent emotion. They live in northern tundra/taiga **north of Arataure**, capital **Hara-Ur**; Arhîn and Durhîn **hate one another**.

The **Cehîn**, formerly the Aurësén of **Ytharivandor**, secured protection from the Withering via Lord Thraen's pact with **Myrhufrun**, acquiring **antlers, leaf-like skin and piercing golden eyes**. The Arhîn consider the change an affront to heritage; the Cehîn regard the Arhîn as arrogant and foolish. Relations are troubled, though **trade and diplomacy continue**. See their [culture PDD](Cehin-Culture-PDD.md).
