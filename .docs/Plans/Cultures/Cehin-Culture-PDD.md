# 1. Purpose and Authority

The **Cehîn (Weald Children)** are a distinct contemporary people descended from the **Aurësén**. This is their culture-specific record under the [Culture Design PDD](../Culture-Design-PDD.md). Shared history of the Grey Ones, the Serpent War, the Night Serpent and the Ertniiagra is documented in [World Lore and History](../World-Lore-and-History-PDD.md).

Use **Established** for stated canon, **Proposed** for design interpretation and **Further Consideration Required** for open questions. Cultural lore does not prescribe gameplay class, playable race, magic rules or actual in-engine assets.

# 2. Established History and Identity

## 2.1 Origin in the Aurësén

The Cehîn were **not a separate people before the Serpent War**. They were Aurësén living in **Ytharivandor (the Ancient Forest)**, a forest on the **southern edge of the Great Forests**.

At the conclusion of the Serpent War, the ancient territorial ruler **Lord Thraen** negotiated a pact with **Myrhufrun, the Stag King** (one of the **six Summer Court Grey Ones**). The pact protected those who accepted it from the **Withering**, but changed their appearance, with **greenish skin pigmentation featuring a visible leaf-vein texture** and **piercing golden eyes**. Skin pigmentation varies between individuals, from **pale yellow-green to darker forest-green**; the distribution of tones is not yet defined. **Cehîn hair is coarser than that of their Aurësén ancestors, with colours shifted toward an autumnal palette.** The precise range of hair colours and textures remains to be specified. Both the pigmentation and the vein-like pattern are characteristic of their leaf-like skin. **Some ordinary Cehîn also develop small natural antlers or antler-like stubs**, though not all do. Their presence and size reflect **individual variation**, not an established relationship with age or ancestry. These are **much smaller than Lord Thraen's full pair of antlers**. **The Cehîn retain the same underlying physical proportions and facial anatomy as the Arhîn**: Myrhufrun's pact did not change their basic humanoid build or facial structure. Leaf-like skin, piercing golden eyes and occasional small antlers are distinguishing features, not evidence of a separate set of body proportions.

The Cehîn were obliged to **protect Ytharivandor** and effectively became the Stag King's **indentured servants**. These terms did **not** forbid them from leaving the forest: the Cehîn **may leave**, although culturally they rarely choose to.

A small number of Aurësén **refused the pact**. They were subsequently **sacrificed by Lord Thraen and his followers**, acting **independently of the pact's terms**. Do not describe their deaths as a price Myrhufrun imposed.

## 2.2 Government and popular legitimacy

The Cehîn **govern themselves**, with **Lord Thraen** as their leader. Thraen was already the lord of this territory before the Serpent War; his leadership is ancient and continuous, not a recently established royal bloodline. His authority is accepted largely **because he secured the community's protection from the Withering**, rather than through routine coercive rule. He combines **secular leadership** with the supreme religious office of **High Priest of Myrhufrun**. **Thraen bears a pair of antlers emerging directly from his head and the spiral flame rune of Myrhufrun on his forehead**. **He acquired both features when he first made his pact with Myrhufrun at the end of the Serpent War**, rather than receiving them later upon becoming High Priest. These are aspects of his appearance, not simply a separate antler crown or applied insignia; whether the rune glows, and any additional regalia, remain undescribed.

**Myrhufrun is their patron deity** and the recipient of their pact. He lives **at the Moonlight Well among the Cehîn**, but **does not participate in or govern any aspect of Cehîn civilisation**, including its political and military affairs. These remain entirely under Cehîn authority, principally Lord Thraen. His direct communication with Thraen and the priesthood must not be mistaken for engagement in governing or organising Cehîn society.

**Myrhufrun wanders freely throughout Ytharivandor** rather than remaining in the Moonlight Well's glade. **Outsiders who encounter him while he wanders do not survive**; for them, the only established safe occasion to see him is **at the Moonlight Well during special ceremonies**. **The Cehîn themselves are protected during such encounters, provided they show him respect.** They **usually prostrate themselves before him** as an expression of that respect. Prostration is customary, not yet established as a universally mandatory rite or the sole acceptable response. The consequences for a disrespectful Cehîn remain undefined. Fatal encounters may involve Myrhufrun personally hunting and killing intruders, a supernatural effect of his presence, or both; these are not regarded as meaningfully separate explanations at this stage. Do not impose a particular automatic gaze effect or hunting sequence as canon. His ceremonial appearances and protection of respectful Cehîn do not imply participation in governing their civilisation.

As **High Priest of Myrhufrun**, **Lord Thraen exercises authority over the priesthood's religious practices** and **appoints its senior priests**. His authority is not limited to clerical appointments. Subordinate offices, specific rites and duties, wider laws, succession rules, political councils and boundaries of Thraen's rule remain open.

# 3. Geography, Settlements and Architecture

## 3.1 Ytharivandor

Ytharivandor is the **Ancient Forest**, bordering the **Arhîn territories to its north** and the **Moveli to its south**. The southern boundary includes **mountains**. Moveli encroachment across these mountains periodically produces **border skirmishes**.

Most Cehîn live in **small woodland enclaves**, rather than numerous large urban settlements. There is **one major settlement**, **Te'tutivae**, at the Moonlight Well. It is **Lord Thraen's permanent seat**.

**Ytharivandor was among the earliest regions settled by the Aurësén** and preserves **some of their oldest architecture**. Ancient Aurësén masonry is incorporated **throughout the smaller Cehîn woodland settlements**, not only at Te'tutivae; the settlements combine that inherited built fabric with trees and natural materials. **The Cehîn generally allow these ancient structures to decay and become overgrown rather than actively restoring them.** They regard architectural decay as **a natural process in which the forest reclaims the remnants of an old, defunct civilisation**. Ruins are incorporated into the living settlements without an expectation that their original condition should be preserved. The amount of surviving masonry and its condition vary by location and have not been specified.

## 3.2 Te'tutivae — Established design language

Te'tutivae's **lower architectural levels** use **ancient, mossy brickwork** reminiscent of historical Aurësén construction. Its **upper levels** incorporate **trees**. Both ancient trees naturally integrated into the settlement and **magically shaped living trees** are present.

The design must visibly join an inherited built foundation to a predominantly woodland upper environment. **New Cehîn buildings are created primarily through druidic treeshaping**, rather than conventional timber framing or new masonry. **Treeshaping is a rare druidic talent; druids who specialise in it are highly respected and sought after** for their ability to form living trees into architecture. **Treeshaping works on particular living trees that possess a sentient spirit.** The Cehîn can **plant these trees and shape them progressively as they grow**, or work with already established trees. The structures are formed from living growth, **not conjured as complete buildings from nothing**. These sentient trees **cannot simply be compelled to submit to treeshaping**: treeshapers must **convince them and bargain with them** before shaping their growth. **Established examples of bargains** include growing particular companion plants nearby or applying specific protections to the tree. The trees' precise modes of communication, other possible bargain terms, and the time required for shaping remain open. This construction practice applies throughout Cehîn settlements, not only Te'tutivae. The exact engineering, density, tree species, floor heights, bridges, shaping limitations and housing plans are not established. Avoid substituting either a uniformly pristine Aurësén city or a wholly timberless tree village.

**Proposed interpretation:** legible old brick bases, moss and vegetation delivered predominantly through materials/shaders, living trunks rising through ancient courts, elevated timber or living-wood walkways and compact residential structures where construction is plausible. These are art proposals, not mandated individual building assets.

## 3.3 The Moonlight Well — Established

The Moonlight Well remains **exposed to the sky deep within a wooded glade** in Te'tutivae. It is a **natural pool surrounded by stone** with a **pale blue opalescent quality**. Its water has a **silvery surface** and possesses **healing properties**.

It is a world-linked magical font, not a portal to the Grey. The Aurësén originally emerged into the world there. Since the Terhat-Ilu, its connection to the physical world also links it to the Night Serpent. See the shared lore document for the actual mechanism and competing beliefs.

**Access is heavily restricted by Lord Thraen**. Entry is ordinarily permitted to relevant **Cehîn religious officials** and **participants in the Ertniiagra**. Others may receive permission **under exceptional circumstances**. It is neither a public healing spring nor a freely accessible pilgrimage destination.

# 4. Religion, Druidism and Sacrifice

## 4.1 Myrhufrun and the priesthood — Established

**Myrhufrun's appearance:** He resembles an **enormous stag**, marked by a **bright golden rune on his forehead** and **bright golden eyes**. **The Cehîn recognise the forehead rune as Myrhufrun's own symbol**: it is a **spiral flame rune**, expressing his identity and patronage rather than an unknown religious mystery. The precise rendering of the spiral flame and any additional meaning remain unspecified. In encounters within Ytharivandor, observers typically perceive **only his vast silhouette**, with the golden eyes and forehead rune shining visibly. The proportions of his body, the shape of his antlers and any additional features remain undescribed; do not invent a humanoid form or assume his whole body is illuminated.

The Stag King is the **Cehîn's patron deity**. **Myrhufrun communicates directly with Lord Thraen and the Cehîn priesthood**; despite dwelling at the Moonlight Well, he is not generally isolated from his religious representatives. The Cehîn possess an **organised priesthood dedicated to Myrhufrun, whose priests are also practitioners of druidism**. Priesthood and druidic practice are therefore **integrated**, not separate institutions, among the Cehîn. Their druidic traditions include **darker practices**, including ritual sacrifice. **Lord Thraen is the High Priest of Myrhufrun**, directs the priesthood's religious practices and **appoints its senior priests**. Whether every Cehîn druid belongs to the priesthood is not established. **Priests customarily wear a ceremonial crown and collar made of antlers. Wearing antler ornaments is otherwise forbidden to those outside the priesthood.** This restriction concerns *worn antlers*, as distinct from the naturally occurring antler-like features of the Cehîn themselves. **Thraen is visibly distinguished by his own pair of head-growing antlers and the rune of Myrhufrun on his forehead.** This does not establish whether he wears the ordinary priests' antler crown or collar as well. Lower clerical appointment methods, internal hierarchy, other garments, shrine requirements and precise priestly duties remain open.

## 4.2 The Ertniiagra — Established

The Cehîn regard the **Ertniiagra** as profoundly important, as do the centaurs. It is the generational journey in which chosen **western and eastern centaurs** are sacrificed at the Moonlight Well. The public explanation is that the rite helps preserve the supposed imprisonment of the Night Serpent, consistent with the Tinithas' lie.

**Hidden truth:** the sacrifices deliver **sylvan blood** to the Well. Kabithh uses that blood to suppress the Night Serpent from within its mind. The Cehîn **do not know the hidden mechanism**; they share the conventional understanding that sacrifices are needed to keep the Night Serpent bound. The older Arhîn theology of soul passage through the Well should not be conflated with the Ertniiagra.

## 4.3 Other Cehîn sacrifices — Established

The Cehîn continue **ritual sacrifice** in the Ninth Age. The killing of their own people who refused the original pact was **exceptional**, not their ordinary present practice. Today they **typically sacrifice outsiders**, most often **travellers and intruders who enter Ytharivandor**, rather than sending capture parties specifically to raid foreign lands. Outsiders commonly **do not know the danger beforehand** because the Cehîn do not actively publicise it with warnings.

The ordinary outsider sacrifices are a **separate practice** from the Ertniiagra's specific centaur rite; neither should be described as a blanket licence for any traveller to enter the Well's restricted glade.

**Only the organised priesthood of Myrhufrun conducts ritual sacrifices.** Ordinary Cehîn do not conduct such sacrifices independently. The rites' precise purposes, frequency, selection procedures, sacred objects and supernatural effects have not been established. Do not invent them.

# 5. Foreign Relations and Trade

## 5.1 The Arhîn — Established

Cehîn–Arhîn relations are **troubled**. The Arhîn see the transformation into antlered, leaf-skinned descendants as an **affront to Aurësén heritage**. The Cehîn consider the Arhîn **arrogant and bigoted**, and regard them as fools for not seeking a similar escape from the Withering. These attitudes coexist with **diplomatic and trading relations**; they do not imply permanent open warfare.

## 5.2 The Moveli — Established

The **Moveli** are the principal southern neighbours. They sometimes **encroach across the mountains** into Cehîn lands, provoking skirmishes along the border. The Cehîn nevertheless maintain trading/diplomatic relationships with neighbouring civilisations.

## 5.3 Export economy — Established

The Cehîn **typically export forest goods**. This serves a strategic social purpose: supplying outsiders with forest products **reduces their incentive to enter the sacred forest themselves**. Specific goods, prices, trade routes, diplomatic offices and foreign concessions remain undefined.

# 6. Visual and Asset Identity

**Established:** Aurësén descent; **greenish, leaf-veined skin**, **coarser hair in autumnal colours**, and piercing gold eyes, with **small natural antlers or stubs on some individuals** (not universal); compact woodland enclaves; Te'tutivae's mossy Aurësén-like lower brickwork and living-tree upper architecture; the silvery Well in an opalescent-ringed glade; organised Myrhufrun priesthood and darker druidic ritual traditions. **Myrhufrun** is a gigantic stag, typically glimpsed as a dark silhouette punctuated by **luminous golden eyes** and a **luminous golden spiral flame rune on his forehead**. **The Cehîn reproduce this rune on shrines, ceremonial clothing and religious objects**; it is not reserved solely for depictions of Myrhufrun himself. **Priestly attire includes an antler crown and antler collar**, both forbidden as worn adornments to non-priests. **Lord Thraen** has a pair of antlers emerging from his head and the **spiral flame rune of Myrhufrun on his forehead**; the rune's colour or luminosity on Thraen has not been specified.

**Established clothing materials and appearance:** the Cehîn primarily wear garments made from **woven plant fibres and other natural materials**, rather than retaining the refined textile traditions of their Aurësén ancestors. Garments are **loose and draped, obscuring the wearer's body shape and creating a spectral silhouette** rather than a fitted woodland-traveller outline. Their clothing palette includes **muted greens, browns and autumnal tones**, with **highlights produced using natural colour dyes**; pale tones may be used in ritual contexts, but the exact distribution by occasion is not yet established. Exact garment patterns, weaving techniques and distinctions between ordinary and ceremonial cuts remain open. The priesthood's customary antler crown and collar remain established regalia.

**Proposed only:** botanical adornments, restrained old Aurësén geometric forms preserved in ancestral objects, compact buildings supported by ancient masonry, naturalistic living-wood elements and distinctive druidic ritual props. Avoid copying the fully wild animal-hide identity of Arhîn occultist naturalists for every Cehîn; do not imply that their clothing uses refined Aurësén textiles as its ordinary baseline.

Potential asset categories:
- Cehîn head variants with and without small natural antlers or stubs, skin treatments and hair variants;
- small woodland dwellings and ancient brick bases;
- natural and shaped living-tree building components;
- Lord Thraen's seat and organised priesthood spaces (specific shapes open);
- Moonlight Well pool, opalescent stone and surrounding forest glade;
- forest trade goods and historically grounded domestic objects;
- **spiral flame rune** motifs for Cehîn shrines, ceremonial clothing and religious objects;
- **antler crowns and collars**, as restricted priestly ceremonial regalia.

# 7. Further Consideration Required

- Cehîn population, household structure, occupations and relation of priesthood to social hierarchy.
- The town plan, building typologies and governance facilities of Te'tutivae, as opposed to the smaller enclaves.
- How much of the old Aurësén architecture survives, and specific rules for magic shaping living trees.
- Distinct secular, priestly and druidic appearance, clergy vestments and ritual symbols.
- Religious doctrine, sacrifices, ritual frequency, practices and consequences beyond the established examples.
- How visitors obtain Well permission, the authority of guards/priests and the role of the Ertniiagra.
- The degree to which Myrhufrun's protection is conditional and the impact of his possible death.
- Names and identities of specific Moveli border posts and Cehîn enclave sites.
- Any formal diplomatic/treaty arrangements, forest goods and trade institutions.
