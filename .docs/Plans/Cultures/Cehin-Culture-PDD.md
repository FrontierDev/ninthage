# 1. Purpose and Authority

The **Cehîn (Weald Children)** are a distinct contemporary people descended from the **Aurësén**. This is their culture-specific record under the [Culture Design PDD](../Culture-Design-PDD.md). Shared history of the Grey Ones, the Serpent War, the Night Serpent and the Ertniiagra is documented in [World Lore and History](../World-Lore-and-History-PDD.md).

Use **Established** for stated canon, **Proposed** for design interpretation and **Further Consideration Required** for open questions. Cultural lore does not prescribe gameplay class, playable race, magic rules or actual in-engine assets.

# 2. Established History and Identity

## 2.1 Origin in the Aurësén

The Cehîn were **not a separate people before the Serpent War**. They were Aurësén living in **Ytharivandor (the Ancient Forest)**, a forest on the **southern edge of the Great Forests**.

At the conclusion of the Serpent War, the ancient territorial ruler **Lord Thraen** negotiated a pact with **Myrhufrun, the Stag King** (one of the **six Summer Court Grey Ones**). The pact protected those who accepted it from the **Withering**, but changed their appearance, with **greenish skin pigmentation featuring a visible leaf-vein texture** and **piercing golden eyes**. Skin pigmentation varies between individuals, from **pale yellow-green to darker forest-green**; the distribution of tones is not yet defined. **Cehîn hair is coarser than that of their Aurësén ancestors, with colours shifted toward an autumnal palette.** The precise range of hair colours and textures remains to be specified. Both the pigmentation and the vein-like pattern are characteristic of their leaf-like skin. **Some ordinary Cehîn also develop small natural antlers or antler-like stubs**, though not all do. Their presence and size reflect **individual variation**, not an established relationship with age or ancestry. These are **much smaller than Lord Thraen's full pair of antlers**. **The Cehîn retain the same underlying physical proportions and facial anatomy as the Arhîn**: Myrhufrun's pact did not change their basic humanoid build or facial structure. Leaf-like skin, piercing golden eyes and occasional small antlers are distinguishing features, not evidence of a separate set of body proportions.

The Cehîn were obliged to **protect Ytharivandor** and effectively became the Stag King's **indentured servants**. These terms did **not** forbid them from leaving the forest: the Cehîn **may leave**, although culturally they rarely choose to.

A small number of Aurësén **refused the pact**. They were subsequently **sacrificed by Lord Thraen and his followers**, acting **independently of the pact's terms**. Do not describe their deaths as a price Myrhufrun imposed.

## 2.2 Government and popular legitimacy

The Cehîn **govern themselves**, with **Lord Thraen** as their leader. Thraen was already the lord of this territory before the Serpent War; his leadership is ancient and continuous, not a recently established royal bloodline. His authority is accepted largely **because he secured the community's protection from the Withering**, rather than through routine coercive rule. He combines **secular leadership** with the supreme religious office of **High Priest of Myrhufrun**. **Thraen bears a pair of antlers emerging directly from his head and the spiral flame rune of Myrhufrun on his forehead**. **He acquired both features when he first made his pact with Myrhufrun at the end of the Serpent War**, rather than receiving them later upon becoming High Priest. These are aspects of his appearance, not simply a separate antler crown or applied insignia; whether the rune glows, and any additional regalia, remain undescribed.

**Myrhufrun is their patron deity** and the recipient of their pact. He lives **at the Moonlight Well among the Cehîn**, but **does not participate in or govern any aspect of Cehîn civilisation**, including its political and military affairs. These remain entirely under Cehîn authority, principally Lord Thraen. His direct communication with Thraen and the priesthood must not be mistaken for engagement in governing or organising Cehîn society.

**Myrhufrun wanders freely throughout Ytharivandor** rather than remaining in the Moonlight Well's glade. **Outsiders who encounter him while he wanders do not survive**; for them, the only established safe occasion to see him is **at the Moonlight Well during special ceremonies**. **The Cehîn themselves are protected during such encounters, provided they show him respect.** They **usually prostrate themselves before him** as an expression of that respect. Prostration is customary, not yet established as a universally mandatory rite or the sole acceptable response. The consequences for a disrespectful Cehîn remain undefined. Fatal encounters may involve Myrhufrun personally hunting and killing intruders, a supernatural effect of his presence, or both; these are not regarded as meaningfully separate explanations at this stage. Do not impose a particular automatic gaze effect or hunting sequence as canon. His ceremonial appearances and protection of respectful Cehîn do not imply participation in governing their civilisation.

As **High Priest of Myrhufrun**, **Lord Thraen exercises authority over the priesthood's religious practices** and **appoints its senior priests**. His authority is not limited to clerical appointments. Subordinate offices, specific rites and duties, wider laws, succession rules, political councils and boundaries of Thraen's rule remain open.

# 3. Geography, Settlements and Architecture

## 3.1 Ytharivandor

Ytharivandor is the **Ancient Forest**, bordering the **Arhîn territories to its north** and the **Moveli to its south**. The southern boundary includes **mountains**. Moveli encroachment across these mountains periodically produces **border skirmishes**.

Most Cehîn live in **small woodland enclaves**, rather than numerous large urban settlements. There is **one major settlement**, **Te'tutivae**, at the Moonlight Well. It is **Lord Thraen's permanent seat**.

**Ytharivandor was among the earliest regions settled by the Aurësén** and preserves **some of their oldest architecture**. Ancient Aurësén masonry is incorporated **throughout the smaller Cehîn woodland settlements**, not only at Te'tutivae; the settlements combine that inherited built fabric with trees and natural materials. **The Cehîn generally allow these ancient structures to decay and become overgrown rather than actively restoring them.** They regard architectural decay as **a natural process in which the forest reclaims the remnants of an old, defunct civilisation**. Ruins are incorporated into the living settlements without an expectation that their original condition should be preserved. The amount of surviving masonry and its condition vary by location and have not been specified.

## 3.2 Te'tutivae — Established design language

Te'tutivae's **lower architectural levels** use **ancient, mossy brickwork** reminiscent of historical Aurësén construction. Its **upper levels** incorporate **trees**. Both ancient trees naturally integrated into the settlement and **magically shaped living trees** are present.

The design must visibly join an inherited built foundation to a predominantly woodland upper environment. **New Cehîn buildings are created primarily through druidic treeshaping**, rather than conventional timber framing or new masonry. **Treeshaping is a rare druidic talent; druids who specialise in it are highly respected and sought after** for their ability to form living trees into architecture. **Treeshaping works on particular living trees that possess a sentient spirit.** These trees are **not a naturally sentient native species**: **Myrhufrun first awakened them during the Second Age, millennia ago**, long before the present Cehîn practice of treeshaping. Although Myrhufrun was responsible for their original awakening millennia ago, **Cehîn druids can awaken new spirits in trees themselves**; Myrhufrun does **not** need to awaken each new tree individually. **Awakening a new tree spirit is a rare gift limited to specialist druids**, not an ability shared by most trained Cehîn druids. **Spirit-awakening and treeshaping are distinct druidic talents or specialisations, but a sufficiently gifted druid can possess both.** Neither practice should be treated as a direct specification of the playable **Naturalist class**: these are cultural and worldbuilding traditions, not automatic class abilities or requirements. **Awakening does not grant the tree an instantly fully formed mind**: its sentience and individual personality **develop gradually as the tree grows**. A tree's intelligence **eventually reaches a relatively stable level of maturity**, rather than increasing indefinitely throughout its potentially centuries-long life. Whether any tree can be awakened, how awakening is performed, and the rate or age at which mental maturity is reached remain undefined.

**Established psychology of sentient trees:** mature tree spirits are **intelligent, individual beings, but fundamentally unlike Cehîn or other humanoid minds**. Their consciousness is shaped by being rooted in one place and living through decades, seasons and changing relationships with the surrounding forest, rather than by travel, property, social advancement or immediate personal ambition.

- **Different experience of time:** they regard the passing of seasons, the health of the land and changes unfolding across generations as more significant than many short-lived events. This does not make them unresponsive: imminent injury, fire or an urgent breach of trust can provoke immediate action.
- **Place-bound perception and identity:** their sense of self includes roots, canopy, the soil and water that sustain them, neighbouring plants, and the animals that shelter within them. Changes to this living environment may feel intensely personal rather than merely practical.
- **Unfamiliar priorities:** light, water, room for roots, nearby species, ecological continuity, memory and the freedom to follow natural growth can outweigh comfort, convenience, status, wealth or the wishes of a household.
- **Nonhuman emotions:** trees can form attachments, preferences and aversions, and experience contentment, distress, curiosity, anger or grief, but these do not necessarily resemble humanoid feelings in intensity, expression, duration or cause. A tree may mourn the removal of a neighbouring grove more deeply than the death of an individual occupant it has known for years.
- **Memory tied to the land:** they tend to interpret history through changes in their surroundings—fires, clearings, ruined buildings, migrations, seasons of abundance and loss—rather than through the political chronologies or family histories that preoccupy the Cehîn. The oldest surviving spirits may remember Ytharivandor and Aurësén civilisation as they were in the **Second Age and later**, making them rare first-hand witnesses to that period. **A tree's memories begin at its own awakening and cannot extend earlier**, even if it grew before it became sentient; thus no tree has first-hand memories of events predating the original Second Age awakening. Trees awakened by druids in later periods have correspondingly shorter personal memories. **Cehîn druids occasionally consult ancient tree spirits to recover knowledge of the Aurësén past**, but such consultation is exceptional rather than a routine historical method. **Most of the time these memories are considered too fragmented, alien in perspective and difficult to interpret to serve as reliable historical records.** Their testimony can be ancient and valuable without being a conventional historical account.
- **Distinct moral perspective:** a tree need not share humanoid assumptions about ownership, urgency or the sanctity of constructed dwellings. It may consider reclaiming a structure or allowing masonry to decay wholly natural, even when that disturbs its occupants. **Bargains still matter:** a spirit can understand a promise, negotiate precise conditions and respond to a violation according to its seriousness.
- **Strong individuality:** some spirits are accommodating, inquisitive or fond of their occupants; others are solitary, exacting, possessive of their space or indifferent to humanoid affairs. The differences are not simply human personalities transplanted into trees.

**Treeshaping implication:** rare specialist druids are respected not only because they can wield growth-shaping magic but because they can **understand, interpret and negotiate with an alien form of intelligence**. Telepathic conversation permits meaningful exchange, yet does not erase the differences in priorities or ways of thinking. Their relationship is therefore one of negotiated coexistence, not domestication. The Cehîn can **plant these trees and shape them progressively as they grow**, or work with already established trees. The structures are formed from living growth, **not conjured as complete buildings from nothing**. These sentient trees **cannot simply be compelled to submit to treeshaping**: treeshapers must **convince them and bargain with them** before shaping their growth. **Established treeshaping bargains:** each sentient tree has its own personality and negotiates its own conditions; **no individual tree necessarily demands all of these**. Terms can be ecological, protective, social, ceremonial or concerned with the tree's long-term freedom. Recognised possible demands include:

1. **Companion species:** specific flowers, fungi, shrubs or other trees must be planted and sustained nearby.
2. **Protection from harm:** boundaries against fire, axes, excavation or specified magical interference.
3. **Access to sunlight:** buildings must preserve the tree's preferred canopy exposure even where this constrains the architecture.
4. **Sacred wildlife:** selected birds, insects or woodland creatures must be protected and permitted to nest or live within the shaped tree.
5. **Unrestricted roots:** foundations, tunnels and paths must not encroach upon a specified root area.
6. **Seasonal rest:** the tree must be left unshaped and undisturbed during a chosen season.
7. **Companionship:** another sentient tree must be planted nearby, or a designated druid must visit and converse with the tree.
8. **Preservation of memory:** inhabitants must keep particular histories, recite stories or maintain an inherited tradition.
9. **Protection of offspring:** the Cehîn must cultivate and guard a number of the tree's seedlings.
10. **Freedom to reclaim its form:** after a period of service as a building, the tree may return to natural growth, even if this eventually displaces occupants.
11. **Sacred boundaries:** specified branches, hollows or parts of the trunk must remain untouched.
12. **Protection of ancient ruins:** surrounding Aurësén masonry must be allowed to decay naturally rather than repaired, dismantled or cleared.

**Consequences:** negotiated obligations can shape building plans, movement routes, lighting, tree placement, household customs, maintenance and the eventual abandonment of structures. These are established **possible types of demands**, not fixed requirements for every construction. **Responses to broken bargains depend on the severity of the violation**; no single automatic punishment applies. **For a severe violation, the sentient tree can deliberately reshape or destroy the building it forms, potentially endangering its inhabitants.** The specific range of consequences and how a tree assesses severity are not yet established. **Only druids with the requisite magical ability can communicate directly with sentient trees**; ordinary Cehîn inhabitants cannot converse with them. This makes druids necessary intermediaries in negotiations and ongoing relations between a tree and its occupants. Communication between suitably gifted druids and sentient trees is **telepathic**, rather than spoken or conveyed solely through vague impressions. The enforcement of promises and the time required for shaping remain open. This construction practice applies throughout Cehîn settlements, not only Te'tutivae. The exact engineering, density, tree species, floor heights, bridges, shaping limitations and housing plans are not established. Avoid substituting either a uniformly pristine Aurësén city or a wholly timberless tree village.

**Proposed interpretation:** legible old brick bases, moss and vegetation delivered predominantly through materials/shaders, living trunks rising through ancient courts, elevated timber or living-wood walkways and compact residential structures where construction is plausible. These are art proposals, not mandated individual building assets.

## 3.3 The Moonlight Well — Established

The Moonlight Well remains **exposed to the sky deep within a wooded glade** in Te'tutivae. It is a **natural pool surrounded by stone** with a **pale blue opalescent quality**. Its water has a **silvery surface** and possesses **healing properties**.

It is a world-linked magical font, not a portal to the Grey. The Aurësén originally emerged into the world there. Since the Terhat-Ilu, its connection to the physical world also links it to the Night Serpent. See the shared lore document for the actual mechanism and competing beliefs.

**Access is heavily restricted by Lord Thraen**. Entry is ordinarily permitted to relevant **Cehîn religious officials** and **participants in the Ertniiagra**. Others may receive permission **under exceptional circumstances**, including candidates admitted for a formal pact ceremony. It is neither a public healing spring nor a freely accessible pilgrimage destination.

**Role in the Cehîn transformation (established truth):** the rite conducted by Thraen and the priesthood, together with Myrhufrun's transformative power, initiates the change, but **drinking water from the Moonlight Well is essential to complete it**. Without drinking the water, an incompletely transformed candidate becomes a **mindless husk**, not a fully conscious Cehîn.

**Hidden consequence (author-facing):** drinking the Well's water also creates a vulnerability that **would allow Kabithh to take complete control of the Cehîn's minds and actions if her plan to have Myrhufrun killed succeeds**. This would be **total domination, not merely influence over their emotions or desires while they retain free will**. **Under Kabithh's control, the Cehîn become mindless puppets incapable of independent thought**, rather than retaining their intelligence, memories and individual personalities as conscious but compelled subjects. This state is distinct from the mindless husks produced when the original transformation is left incomplete by failing to drink the Well's water. **Kabithh's domination is reversible in principle: where the Cehîn can be recovered, breaking her control can restore their original intelligence, memories and individual personalities.** In practice, the severe Withering caused by Myrhufrun's death also needs to be addressed; liberation from domination alone does not guarantee a viable recovery for a partially Withered Cehîn. **The interval of domination is a complete blank in their memories**: even after recovering their intelligence and personalities, they retain **no recollection of their actions or experiences while controlled**. **Myrhufrun's death permanently ends the protection from the Withering conferred by his pact, but does not reverse the Cehîn's physical transformation.** Thus, any surviving Cehîn freed from Kabithh's domination remain visibly Cehîn, **without their former immunity to the Withering**; whether they can regain their minds and function independently depends on the success of efforts to reverse the Withering. **The Withering begins immediately upon Myrhufrun's death and progresses rapidly**, rather than developing gradually as among the Arhîn. **Kabithh's domination does not suppress the Withering.** While controlled, the Cehîn rapidly deteriorate but **do not completely disappear**: they remain in a **partially Withered, corporeal state comparable to that of the Durhîn**, yet **without independent control of their minds or actions**, serving as Kabithh's mindless puppets. **This partial Withering can be carefully reversed, but recovery is difficult and most affected Cehîn would likely be lost.** Breaking Kabithh's control alone is not sufficient to ensure the survival or restoration of the already Withered. **The restored Annokal offers a remedy for the Withering to all Aurësén-descended peoples, including the Cehîn, Arhîn and Durhîn, but each Cehîn must accept the Gift to benefit from it.** If restored and accepted before Myrhufrun's death, it would protect the recipient after the Stag King's pact-based protection ceased. If restored afterward, it can reverse the Withering of surviving Cehîn who accept it. **Reactivation alone does not automatically confer protection on all Cehîn.** **A Cehîn under Kabithh's domination cannot accept the Annokal while mindless and compelled; they must first be freed from her control so that they can choose to accept the Gift themselves.** **How that liberation can be achieved is presently undetermined**: it has not been established whether powerful magic could free an individual Cehîn, or whether Kabithh must first be defeated or her link to the Night Serpent severed. No third party can accept the Gift on their behalf. Consequently, the liberation of surviving Cehîn is a prerequisite for their protection and restoration through the Annokal. **An individual Cehîn who accepts the restored Annokal gains immediate protection from the Withering**, while **their distinctive Cehîn appearance gradually returns to the original Aurësén form**. This gradual reversion is caused by accepting the Gift, not simply by Myrhufrun's death; the exact duration and intermediate stages remain undefined. **Most could still be lost if the Withering advances too far before restoration and acceptance are achieved**; this prospect does not mean the restored Annokal works only for some surviving Cehîn. The precise procedure, conditions for successful recovery and physical appearance of the partially Withered remain undecided. **This vulnerability is inherent in the Well's connection to the Night Serpent**, not a curse or modification deliberately introduced into the Well by Kabithh; she intends to exploit a pre-existing property of its magic. This is a future conditional consequence, **not an event that has already occurred**. **Neither Lord Thraen nor Myrhufrun is aware of this vulnerability**. Neither knows that the water required to complete the pact could later enable Kabithh to control the Cehîn. Whether other members of the priesthood or any Cehîn know remains undefined. See the [World Lore and History PDD](../World-Lore-and-History-PDD.md).

## 3.4 Living libraries and written records — Established

The Cehîn have a **distinctive written tradition** in which **ink is used to inscribe broad leaves while the leaves remain attached to their living plants**. The leaves themselves serve as the writing surface; the text is not ordinarily presented as writing on detached, dried leaves or bound paper books. Consequently, **Cehîn libraries resemble gardens and orchards**, with living plants functioning as repositories of written knowledge.

This practice is an established form of Cehîn writing and historical recordkeeping. **Library plants are magically maintained**, preserving their living leaves and the inscriptions written upon them rather than relying on periodic copying to replace naturally shed leaves. The plants used for these libraries are **ordinary plant species**, not specialised species bred for magical recordkeeping; their preservation derives from **applied magic**. **Retrieval depends on specialist librarians who know where individual texts are growing**, rather than on plants being systematically arranged by subject and age. These librarians preserve the practical knowledge needed to locate writings amid the living gardens and orchards. **Librarians maintain written catalogues of the locations of texts**, ensuring that knowledge of where records grow can be passed between generations rather than depending solely on instruction or individual memory. **The catalogues themselves are inscribed on living leaves still attached to their plants**, just like the texts they index; they are not portable books or loose records. **The living libraries are generally accessible to ordinary Cehîn**, not restricted to scholars, druids or the priesthood. Specialist librarians assist with locating written works, but their expertise does not imply exclusive access to the collections. Their indexing conventions and how librarians navigate between catalogue plants and the indexed writings remain undecided. Which ordinary species are preferred and the precise nature and limits of the sustaining magic also remain to be determined.

## 3.5 Literacy and oral tradition — Established

**Literacy is not widespread among the Cehîn and is not part of a universal childhood education.** Reading and writing are learned chiefly by people pursuing **scholarly, religious or administrative roles**, including those involved in tending written records. The living libraries remain generally accessible to ordinary Cehîn, but **public access does not imply universal literacy**.

**Oral history is also an important part of Cehîn society**, existing alongside the written tradition of living-leaf libraries. **Storytelling is a widespread communal practice within Cehîn families and settlements**, not a responsibility reserved for a dedicated caste of storytellers or lorekeepers. History and tradition are passed on through ordinary social life. **Cehîn oral histories favour symbolic, mythologised retellings over strict factual accuracy**, preserving cultural and spiritual lessons rather than functioning primarily as literal chronicles. **They portray their Aurësén ancestors as children who had not yet evolved and still depended on the Annokal for protection.** This is the **Cehîn cultural interpretation of their ancestry**, not an objective claim that the Aurësén were biologically immature or unevolved. The inherited dependence on the Annokal is contrasted with the Cehîn's subsequent transformation and pact with Myrhufrun. **In Cehîn cultural memory, the pact marks the definitive moment of their people's collective coming of age**: they regard it as attainment of maturity, **not the start of an evolutionary process still underway**. This is their self-understanding rather than an objective biological developmental sequence. Their specific storytelling customs, ceremonial functions and relationship to written accounts remain to be defined.

## 3.6 Diet and woodland agriculture — Established

The Cehîn maintain a **vegetarian diet** sustained chiefly through **organised woodland agriculture**, rather than hunting or dependence on incidental wild foraging. Their cuisine is **earthy, aromatic and seasonal**, and is neither nutritionally austere nor dependent on conjured food. Grains, roots, legumes, cultivated fungi, nuts, fruits and herbs provide their main foodstuffs. Long-established preparation techniques include slow simmering, stone baking, drying, fermenting legumes, pressing nut oils and preserving fruit; typical flavours are earthy, sour, nutty, herbal and occasionally sweet.

### Agricultural environments — Established

- **Woodland clearings:** numerous relatively small, cultivated patches supply staples needing substantial sunlight, including **Tévar** grain, **Ulreth** roots, beans and other field crops; these do not require the forest to be cleared wholesale.
- **Under-canopy gardens:** herbs, leafy vegetables, shade-tolerant legumes and cultivated fungi grow among retained woodland vegetation, including on living trellises and damp fallen timber.
- **Orchards and nut groves:** cultivated fruit and nut trees provide fresh produce, oils and storable foods. Orchard trees may stand within inhabited settlements, with paths and buildings weaving among them.

Ordinary food plants are **non-sentient**. Gathering fruit or nuts from an awakened sentient tree requires the tree's **consent under its agreement with the Cehîn**, consistent with the established bargain-based relationship between tree spirits and inhabitants. The extent of magical assistance in farming, if any, remains undecided.

### Established crops and foodstuffs

| Cehîn name | Foodstuff | Cultivation, use and characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Tévar** | Woodland grain | Small copper-husked cereal grown in sunny forest clearings; boiled for porridge or ground into coarse flour for flatbread; the principal carbohydrate staple. |
| **Ulreth** | Pale root | Large segmented root vegetable grown in damp forest soil; pale, slightly sweet and nutty flesh; roasted whole, mashed or cooked in stews. |
| **Nivra** | Climbing bean | Shade-tolerant legume trained along living trellises or ordinary trees; dark seeds supply much of the dietary protein; boiled, mashed into paste or fermented. |
| **Saelcap** | Cultivated forest fungus | Broad, layered savoury mushroom raised on damp fallen timber; eaten fresh or dried for winter stores; central to soups and stews. |
| **Orren** | Oil nut | Large orchard-grown woodland nut; roasted, pressed for cooking oil, ground into flour, or blended into creamy sauces and drinks. |
| **Ithrel** | Silver pear | Small, pale-green, silvery-skinned fruit; fragrant, sweet and juicy; eaten fresh, dried or pressed into syrup for preserving and sweetening. |
| **Vaelis** | Duskberry | Dark violet, sharply tart berry grown around shaded orchard margins; made into preserves, fermented drinks and acidic sauces for nut- or mushroom-rich dishes. |
| **Thirrel** | Forest herb | Broad-leaved, peppery aromatic herb; used in soups, sauces, grain dishes and herbal infusions. |

### Established dishes and preparations

- **Saelvar — mushroom and grain stew:** a substantial everyday household dish of Saelcap mushrooms, Tévar grain, Nivra beans and forest herbs, slowly cooked in large earthenware pots.
- **Ulreth wraps:** thin Tévar flatbreads filled with roasted Ulreth, seasoned Nivra bean paste and fresh Thirrel leaves; practical food for farmers, travellers and woodland workers.
- **The autumn offering:** communal seasonal harvest meal of slow-roasted Ulreth roots, Orren nuts, mushrooms and Vaelis berry sauces, celebrating woodland abundance. The name does **not** by itself establish a formal priestly sacrifice.
- **Ithrel and Orren cakes:** dense nut-flour and Tévar cakes sweetened with Ithrel pear syrup and filled with cooked fruit; served at gatherings and celebrations.

These dishes form the established foundation of Cehîn cuisine, not an exhaustive menu. Exact recipes, seasonal festival dates, agricultural calendars, kitchen arrangements and culinary differences between enclaves and Te'tutivae remain open.

## 3.7 Communal households — Established

Cehîn society is organised primarily around **large communal households formed by extended family groups**, rather than separate small-family homes or mainly voluntary groupings of unrelated families. **Multiple generations of related families share residential space, food preparation and childcare** as part of everyday domestic life.

**Cehîn extended-family households are strongly hierarchical, with the eldest generations holding formal authority over household decisions.** This hierarchy is expressed spatially: **older generations occupy the upper floors**. Cehîn druidic treeshaping can guide neighbouring sentient trees to **connect their canopies**, creating **substantial horizontal living space high above the ground**, rather than limiting each household to a single tree or narrow vertical tower. Connected canopy living spaces can therefore accommodate large multigenerational families. Treeshaping must still respect the established need to negotiate and uphold agreements with the sentient trees involved; the connections are not built through unconstrained magical compulsion.

**Older Cehîn are culturally expected, but not required, to enter the clergy.** **Those who do become priests retain their existing authority over their extended-family households**, including household leadership and decision-making; they are not expected to hand over domestic authority to the next generation upon entering religious service. Clerical and household responsibilities may therefore coexist in the same elder. This expectation does not imply that every household elder is a priest or that age automatically grants clerical office. The exact number of generations sharing a household, allocation of lower levels, succession and division of authority among elders, and how food preparation and childcare duties are assigned remain undecided.

# 4. Religion, Druidism and Sacrifice

## 4.1 Myrhufrun and the priesthood — Established

**Myrhufrun's appearance:** He resembles an **enormous stag**, marked by a **bright golden rune on his forehead** and **bright golden eyes**. **The Cehîn recognise the forehead rune as Myrhufrun's own symbol**: it is a **spiral flame rune**, expressing his identity and patronage rather than an unknown religious mystery. The precise rendering of the spiral flame and any additional meaning remain unspecified. In encounters within Ytharivandor, observers typically perceive **only his vast silhouette**, with the golden eyes and forehead rune shining visibly. The proportions of his body, the shape of his antlers and any additional features remain undescribed; do not invent a humanoid form or assume his whole body is illuminated.

The Stag King is the **Cehîn's patron deity**. **Myrhufrun communicates directly with Lord Thraen and the Cehîn priesthood**; despite dwelling at the Moonlight Well, he is not generally isolated from his religious representatives. The Cehîn possess an **organised priesthood dedicated to Myrhufrun, whose priests are also practitioners of druidism**. Priesthood and druidic practice are therefore **integrated**, not separate institutions, among the Cehîn. Their druidic traditions include **darker practices**, including ritual sacrifice. **Lord Thraen is the High Priest of Myrhufrun**, directs the priesthood's religious practices and **appoints its senior priests**. **Entering the clergy is a customary expectation for older Cehîn, but is voluntary, not compulsory.** An elder may remain outside the priesthood without forfeiting their status as a household elder; priestly office is not automatically conferred by age. **Similarly, an elder who enters the priesthood keeps their household authority rather than relinquishing it to younger relatives.** Whether every Cehîn druid belongs to the priesthood is not established. **Priests customarily wear a ceremonial crown and collar made of antlers. Wearing antler ornaments is otherwise forbidden to those outside the priesthood.** This restriction concerns *worn antlers*, as distinct from the naturally occurring antler-like features of the Cehîn themselves. **Thraen is visibly distinguished by his own pair of head-growing antlers and the rune of Myrhufrun on his forehead.** This does not establish whether he wears the ordinary priests' antler crown or collar as well. Lower clerical appointment methods, internal hierarchy, other garments, shrine requirements and precise priestly duties remain open.

## 4.2 The Ertniiagra — Established

The Cehîn regard the **Ertniiagra** as profoundly important, as do the centaurs. It is the generational journey in which chosen **western and eastern centaurs** are sacrificed at the Moonlight Well. The public explanation is that the rite helps preserve the supposed imprisonment of the Night Serpent, consistent with the Tinithas' lie.

**Hidden truth:** the sacrifices deliver **sylvan blood** to the Well. Kabithh uses that blood to suppress the Night Serpent from within its mind. The Cehîn **do not know the hidden mechanism**; they share the conventional understanding that sacrifices are needed to keep the Night Serpent bound. The older Arhîn theology of soul passage through the Well should not be conflated with the Ertniiagra.

## 4.3 Other Cehîn sacrifices — Established

The Cehîn continue **ritual sacrifice** in the Ninth Age. The killing of their own people who refused the original pact was **exceptional**, not their ordinary present practice. Today they **typically sacrifice outsiders**, most often **travellers and intruders who enter Ytharivandor**, rather than sending capture parties specifically to raid foreign lands. Outsiders commonly **do not know the danger beforehand** because the Cehîn do not actively publicise it with warnings.

The ordinary outsider sacrifices are a **separate practice** from the Ertniiagra's specific centaur rite; neither should be described as a blanket licence for any traveller to enter the Well's restricted glade.

**Only the organised priesthood of Myrhufrun conducts ritual sacrifices.** Ordinary Cehîn do not conduct such sacrifices independently. The rites' precise purposes, frequency, selection procedures, sacred objects and supernatural effects have not been established. Do not invent them.

# 5. Foreign Relations and Trade

## 5.1 The Arhîn — Established

Cehîn–Arhîn relations are **troubled**. The Arhîn see the transformation into antlered, leaf-skinned descendants as an **affront to Aurësén heritage**. The Cehîn consider the Arhîn **arrogant and bigoted**, and regard them as fools for not seeking a similar escape from the Withering. **The Cehîn actively attempt to persuade the Arhîn to seek their own transformation and freedom from dependence on the Annokal**, rather than simply waiting for them to make that choice independently. These efforts reflect the Cehîn belief that their own pact represented a collective coming of age. **They advocate both the possibility of entering into a pact with Myrhufrun and the broader principle that the Arhîn should seek their own path to transformation and freedom from the Withering.** They do not regard Myrhufrun's particular pact as the only conceivable route to such independence. **Myrhufrun is willing to enter into new pacts with individual Arhîn**; his original agreement with Lord Thraen was not an unrepeatable exception. **Arhîn who enter such a pact undergo the same kind of physical transformation as the original Cehîn**, rather than receiving unique physical forms on a pact-by-pact basis. **Newly transformed Arhîn are expected to join Cehîn society in Ytharivandor**, rather than remain in their former Arhîn homes and social affiliations while bound to Myrhufrun. **Newly transformed Arhîn are immediately accepted as full members of Cehîn society**, with no probationary period or lesser social standing imposed on account of their origins. **These newcomers undertake the same pact obligations as the original Cehîn: safeguarding Ytharivandor and serving Myrhufrun.** Their pacts do not establish individually negotiated alternatives to those core duties. **An Arhîn seeking to enter Myrhufrun's pact must travel to the Moonlight Well in Te'tutivae and undergo a formal ceremony conducted by Lord Thraen and the Cehîn priesthood.** The pact is not ordinarily established through an informal private encounter with Myrhufrun elsewhere in Ytharivandor. The ceremony constitutes a sanctioned occasion for the candidate to enter the otherwise restricted Well. **Myrhufrun appears in person at each pact ceremony, but remains at a distance, visible only as a silhouette among the trees surrounding the Moonlight Well.** He does not approach the candidate or appear up close. **Thraen's and the priesthood's ritual, and Myrhufrun's magic, begin the transformation; the candidate must then drink from the Moonlight Well to complete it.** Without the water, the candidate becomes a **mindless husk**. The rite's other actions and requirements remain to be defined. The newcomer's practical adjustment to Cehîn customs also remains open. These attitudes coexist with **diplomatic and trading relations**; they do not imply permanent open warfare.

## 5.2 The Moveli — Established

The **Moveli** are the principal southern neighbours. They sometimes **encroach across the mountains** into Cehîn lands, provoking skirmishes along the border. The Cehîn nevertheless maintain trading/diplomatic relationships with neighbouring civilisations.

## 5.3 Export economy — Established

The Cehîn **typically export forest goods**. This serves a strategic social purpose: supplying outsiders with forest products **reduces their incentive to enter the sacred forest themselves**. Specific goods, prices, trade routes, diplomatic offices and foreign concessions remain undefined.

# 6. Visual and Asset Identity

**Established:** Aurësén descent; **greenish, leaf-veined skin**, **coarser hair in autumnal colours**, and piercing gold eyes, with **small natural antlers or stubs on some individuals** (not universal); compact woodland enclaves; Te'tutivae's mossy Aurësén-like lower brickwork and living-tree upper architecture; the silvery Well in an opalescent-ringed glade; organised Myrhufrun priesthood and darker druidic ritual traditions. **Myrhufrun** is a gigantic stag, typically glimpsed as a dark silhouette punctuated by **luminous golden eyes** and a **luminous golden spiral flame rune on his forehead**. **The Cehîn reproduce this rune on shrines, ceremonial clothing and religious objects**; it is not reserved solely for depictions of Myrhufrun himself. **Priestly attire includes an antler crown and antler collar**, both forbidden as worn adornments to non-priests. **Lord Thraen** has a pair of antlers emerging from his head and the **spiral flame rune of Myrhufrun on his forehead**; the rune's colour or luminosity on Thraen has not been specified.

**Established clothing materials and appearance:** the Cehîn primarily wear garments made from **woven plant fibres and other natural materials**, rather than retaining the refined textile traditions of their Aurësén ancestors. Garments are **loose and draped, obscuring the wearer's body shape and creating a spectral silhouette** rather than a fitted woodland-traveller outline. Their clothing palette includes **muted greens, browns and autumnal tones**, with **highlights produced using natural colour dyes**; pale tones may be used in ritual contexts, but the exact distribution by occasion is not yet established. Exact garment patterns, weaving techniques and distinctions between ordinary and ceremonial cuts remain open. The priesthood's customary antler crown and collar remain established regalia.

**Proposed only:** botanical adornments, restrained old Aurësén geometric forms preserved in ancestral objects, compact buildings supported by ancient masonry, naturalistic living-wood elements and distinctive druidic ritual props. Avoid copying the fully wild animal-hide identity of Arhîn occultist naturalists for every Cehîn; do not imply that their clothing uses refined Aurësén textiles as its ordinary baseline.

Potential asset categories:
- Cehîn head variants with and without small natural antlers or stubs, skin treatments and hair variants;
- small woodland dwellings and ancient brick bases;
- natural and shaped living-tree building components;
- Lord Thraen's seat and organised priesthood spaces (specific shapes open);
- Moonlight Well pool, opalescent stone and surrounding forest glade;
- forest trade goods and historically grounded domestic objects;
- **spiral flame rune** motifs for Cehîn shrines, ceremonial clothing and religious objects;
- **antler crowns and collars**, as restricted priestly ceremonial regalia.
